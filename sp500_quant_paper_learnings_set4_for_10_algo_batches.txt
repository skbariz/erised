S&P 500 QUANT PAPER LEARNINGS - SET 4
Fourth non-overlapping set of 20 papers for planning 10 additional batches of trading algorithms

Purpose:
This file is a chatbot-ready research brief. It summarizes a fourth set of 20 quant trading,
financial modeling, portfolio construction, and backtest-validation papers that were not used as
core papers in Sets 1, 2, or 3. It then turns those papers into 10 practical development batches
for S&P 500 strategy research in Claude Code CLI.

Important:
This is research and software-planning material, not financial advice. Every strategy must be
tested with point-in-time data, realistic transaction costs, conservative execution assumptions,
out-of-sample validation, and explicit records of how many model variants were tested.

================================================================================
HOW SET 4 IS DIFFERENT FROM SETS 1, 2, AND 3
================================================================================

Set 1 focused on the core alpha engine:
- momentum, trend, value, quality, profitability, low beta, volatility management,
  earnings drift, liquidity-aware reversal, pairs/stat-arb, and ML ranking.

Set 2 focused on additional cross-sectional anomalies:
- industry momentum, residual momentum, reversal, seasonality, corporate issuance,
  analyst/insider/short-seller signals, options-informed signals, attention, and sentiment.

Set 3 focused on robustness and hard-data signal families:
- lottery risk, distress, accounting sustainability, R&D/intangibles, customer-supplier links,
  volume/visibility, text tone, index events, fund-flow pressure, and trading-cost survival.

Set 4 focuses on the top-down and portfolio layer:
- equity premium prediction and market exposure timing
- forecast combinations instead of single macro predictors
- consumption-wealth and cross-sectional valuation state variables
- variance risk premium and option-implied expected returns
- model-free implied volatility and option-implied skew
- marketwide liquidity risk and funding-liquidity stress
- liquidity risk in momentum and earnings drift
- mutual-fund flow sentiment and flow-induced return pressure
- robust portfolio construction under estimation error
- backtest-overfitting controls and research governance

The main use of Set 4 is to make your S&P 500 strategy factory better at:
- deciding when to take more or less market exposure
- sizing strategy sleeves by regime
- managing liquidity and crash risk
- building portfolios that do not collapse under estimation error
- rejecting backtests that only look good because too many variants were tried

================================================================================
IMPLEMENTATION WARNINGS FOR THIS SET
================================================================================

1. Macro timing is fragile.
   Equity premium prediction papers disagree. Treat market timing as a cautious exposure scaler,
   not an all-in/all-out switch.

2. Most predictors are low signal-to-noise.
   Use forecast combinations, sign constraints, nonnegative expected return constraints, and
   walk-forward validation.

3. Option-implied variables require careful calendars.
   Align option data by quote date, expiration, moneyness, and option availability. Do not mix
   today's VIX or option surface into yesterday's rebalance.

4. Liquidity and funding stress are regime variables.
   They may not produce daily signals. Use them to size risk, widen no-trade bands, reduce
   leverage, and change turnover limits.

5. Portfolio optimization can overfit faster than alpha signals.
   Start with equal weight, capped equal weight, inverse-vol, shrinkage covariance, and no-short
   constrained minimum variance before using expected-return optimizers.

6. Backtest governance is part of the alpha.
   Save every parameter trial. If you do not know how many strategies were tested, you cannot
   honestly judge the best Sharpe ratio.

01. Goyal and Welch (2008)
Title: A Comprehensive Look at The Empirical Performance of Equity Premium Prediction
Source: https://faculty.washington.edu/ezivot/econ589/goyalWelchRFS2008.pdf

Core idea:
Many popular equity premium predictors look good in-sample but fail out-of-sample. Forecasting the
market return is hard, unstable, and vulnerable to data mining.

Trading translation:
Do not trust a single macro timing variable. Use this paper as the "skeptic baseline" for every
SPY exposure model. A timing overlay must beat the historical average or simple trend/risk rules
out of sample, net of turnover.

Features to implement:
- dividend_yield
- earnings_price
- book_to_market_market
- term_spread
- default_spread
- treasury_bill_rate
- inflation
- net_equity_issuance
- historical_average_equity_premium

Strategy ideas:
- Market-timing benchmark: compare every predictor to historical average return.
- Predictor graveyard report: list variables that work in-sample but fail out-of-sample.
- Exposure scaler only if forecast survives OOS tests and costs.

Main caveats:
- The lesson is not "never time the market"; it is "assume market timing is guilty until proven
  robust."
- Macroeconomic series have revisions and publication lags.
- Avoid tuning sample start dates to make a predictor look good.

02. Campbell and Thompson (2008)
Title: Predicting Excess Stock Returns Out of Sample: Can Anything Beat the Historical Average?
Source: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-7dd1-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b

Core idea:
Weak economic restrictions can improve out-of-sample equity premium forecasts. Examples include
forcing coefficients to have economically sensible signs and setting negative equity premium
forecasts to zero.

Trading translation:
Build constrained forecasts. If a predictor says expected stock excess return is sharply negative
from a variable that should have a positive relationship, clip or reject the signal.

Features to implement:
- constrained_predictor_forecast
- positive_slope_constraint
- nonnegative_equity_premium_forecast
- rolling_oos_r2
- forecast_volatility
- model_turnover

Strategy ideas:
- Constrained macro exposure: scale SPY between 0 and 1.25 based on clipped expected return.
- Forecast sign guardrail: reject coefficient signs that violate prior economics.
- Steady-state valuation forecast: smooth valuation ratios before forecasting.

Main caveats:
- Constraints reduce nonsense but do not create strong signal by themselves.
- Use expanding-window or walk-forward fitting only.
- Forecast evaluation must be out-of-sample.

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v23y2010i2p821-862.html

Core idea:
Combining forecasts from many predictors can outperform relying on any single predictor. Forecast
combination reduces instability and links equity premium forecasts to real economic conditions.

Trading translation:
Use a macro forecast ensemble. A median or trimmed-mean forecast across predictors is often more
stable than the best-looking individual regression.

Features to implement:
- forecast_dp

- forecast_term
- forecast_default
- forecast_inflation
- forecast_net_issuance
- combo_mean_forecast
- combo_median_forecast
- combo_trimmed_forecast
- combo_dispersion

Strategy ideas:
- Combination equity premium forecast.
- Regime confidence score: lower exposure when predictors disagree.
- Predictor contribution report for token-efficient debugging.

Main caveats:
- Forecast combination can still fail if all inputs are weak.
- Store every individual forecast, not just the final combined value.
- Watch for macro data revisions.

Trading translation:

Trading translation:

NEW STRATEGY PRIMITIVES FROM SET 4

Primitive 1: Skeptical macro timing
- Market timing must beat simple historical-average and trend baselines out of sample.

Primitive 2: Constrained forecasts

Primitive 3: Forecast combinations

Primitive 6: Option-implied risk
- Use model-free implied volatility and option-implied skew for risk sizing and crash awareness.

Primitive 7: Liquidity beta
- Measure exposure to marketwide liquidity shocks, not only stock-level trading volume.

Primitive 8: Funding-stress regime
- Reduce gross exposure, leverage, and turnover when market liquidity and funding liquidity are
  both stressed.

Primitive 9: Flow-induced pressure
- Separate genuine alpha momentum from demand pressure caused by fund flows.

Primitive 10: Simple portfolio baselines
- Equal weight, inverse-vol, no-short minimum variance, and shrinkage covariance are mandatory
  benchmarks.

Primitive 11: Overfit-aware research process
- A strategy is not accepted unless its trial count, OOS behavior, costs, and selection bias are
  recorded.

BATCH 1: Equity Premium Forecasting Baselines
Source papers:
- Goyal and Welch (2008)
- Campbell and Thompson (2008)
- Rapach, Strauss, and Zhou (2010)

Objective:
Build a cautious market-timing module that forecasts the S&P 500 equity premium and turns it into
a bounded SPY exposure overlay.

Algorithms:
1. Historical-average equity premium baseline.
2. Individual predictive regressions with walk-forward OOS evaluation.
3. Campbell-Thompson constrained forecasts.
4. Rapach-Strauss-Zhou forecast combination.

Required data:
- Monthly S&P 500 or SPY total returns.
- Treasury bill returns.
- Valuation ratios, interest-rate spreads, inflation, net issuance.
- Data release dates or conservative lags.

- Build `features_equity_premium_predictors.py`.

- Report certainty-equivalent return for a bounded exposure strategy.
