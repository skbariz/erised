# H283: OCFGrowthFactor — YoY Operating Cash Flow Growth

## Hypothesis

Operating cash flow growth is the most reliable test of business quality improvement.
Unlike NI (susceptible to accruals) and GP (captures only top-line), OCF measures actual
cash generated by operations. A firm growing OCF faster than peers is converting earnings
to cash at an increasing rate — the cleanest signal of fundamental improvement.

Signal: ocf_change = (OCF - OCF.shift(252)) / abs(OCF.shift(252)).clip(-5, 5).
Rank descending. Long top-quintile (largest OCF dollar growth). Sector-neutral, monthly rebalance.

Distinction from existing strategies:
- H180: NI change (accrual-based earnings growth)
- H183: CashEarningsDrift uses OCF/NI ratio as a FILTER on NI change, not OCF growth as the signal
- H281: GP dollar growth (not cash flow)
- H282: Revenue dollar growth (top-line, not cash generation)
- H264: Accruals/NOA quality composite (level, not growth)

OCF growth is the cash-flow growth analog to the accrual-filtered earnings signals.

Expected regime behavior:
- **2000–2002**: Tech collapse causes OCF to turn negative; stable consumer names dominate.
- **2008–2009**: OCF disrupted broadly as working capital absorbs revenue shocks.
- **2013–2017**: Sustained OCF expansion; tech and healthcare generate strong signal.
- **2020**: COVID causes OCF spikes in some sectors (tech) and collapses in others (energy).

Success criteria: OOS Sharpe ≥ 0.70, CI lower > 0, H183 corr < 0.85.
Falsification: OCF growth and NI growth rank stocks identically — earnings quality filter is redundant.
