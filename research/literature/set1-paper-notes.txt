================================================================================
THE 20 PAPERS AND IMPLEMENTATION LEARNINGS
================================================================================

01. Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)
Title: Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency
Source: https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/jegadeesh-titman93.pdf
Core idea:
Past intermediate-term winners tend to keep outperforming past losers over 3- to 12-month
horizons. The classic implementation ranks stocks by prior returns, often using 12-month
return excluding the most recent month, then buys winners and shorts losers.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: momentum_12_1 = total_return(t-252 to t-21)
- Rebalance monthly.
- Rank stocks cross-sectionally within the S&P 500 point-in-time universe.
- Variants: top/bottom deciles, quintiles, sector-neutral ranks, volatility-adjusted momentum,
  residual momentum after market/sector regression.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long top 50 momentum names, equal-weighted or inverse-vol weighted.
- Long top quintile / short bottom quintile, beta-neutral.
- Sector-neutral momentum: select top names within each sector.
Main risks:
- Momentum crashes after market rebounds.
- High turnover and crowdedness.
- Signal can overlap heavily with industry momentum and growth exposure.

02. George and Hwang (2004)
Title: The 52-Week High and Momentum Investing
Source: https://www.bauer.uh.edu/TGeorge/papers/gh4-paper.pdf
Core idea:
The closeness of a stock's current price to its 52-week high captures an anchoring-related
momentum effect. Stocks near their 52-week highs may continue to outperform.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: high_52w_ratio = current_price / rolling_252_day_high
- Rank high_52w_ratio descending.
- Use adjusted price series consistently, but avoid using future adjustment data incorrectly.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long top 20% by 52-week-high ratio, avoid stocks that are extended on short-term reversal.
- Combine with 12-1 momentum: buy stocks strong on both measures.
- Sector-neutral 52-week-high rank to avoid concentration in one hot sector.
Main risks:
- Can load on high-beta growth regimes.
- Can chase crowded breakouts if no volatility/risk control.

03. Da, Gurun, and Warachka (2014)
Title: Frog in the Pan: Continuous Information and Momentum
Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v27y2014i7p2171-2218..html
Core idea:
Gradual, persistent price moves can produce stronger continuation than abrupt, jumpy moves.
Investors may underreact more to slow information diffusion than to large discrete events.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: path_smoothness = fraction of positive daily returns during formation window,
  or negative absolute daily jump concentration.
- Combine with 12-1 momentum.
- Prefer stocks with high cumulative momentum and smooth paths.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Smooth momentum: long stocks with strong 6- or 12-month return and many positive days.
- Penalize stocks whose momentum came from one or two large jumps.
- "Quiet trend" strategy: require low realized volatility and high trend consistency.
Main risks:
- Overfitting path definitions.
- Can overlap with low-volatility and quality factors.

04. De Bondt and Thaler (1985)
Title: Does the Stock Market Overreact?
Source: https://breesefine7110.tulane.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/110/2015/10/Debondt-and-Thaler.pdf
Core idea:
Long-horizon losers may subsequently outperform long-horizon winners due to investor
overreaction and reversal. This is different from intermediate-term momentum.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: long_term_reversal = negative prior 3- to 5-year return, optionally excluding
  the most recent 12 months.
- Combine with quality filters to avoid distressed value traps.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long beaten-down large-cap stocks with improving fundamentals and positive recent trend.
- Contrarian basket: bottom prior 3-year return, but only if balance sheet and margins are stable.
- Pair with Piotroski/F-score or quality filter.
Main risks:
- Cheap losers can keep losing.
- Needs drawdown control and careful sector constraints.

05. Fama and French (1993)
Title: Common Risk Factors in the Returns on Stocks and Bonds
Source: https://www.aea.ru/data/pdf/fama1993.pdf
Core idea:
Market, size, and value factors explain much of the cross-section of stock returns.
Value is commonly proxied with book-to-market, and size with market capitalization.
Implementation translation:
- Features: book_to_market, market_cap, market_beta.
- Use as both strategy signals and risk controls.
- Use factor regressions to understand whether an algorithm is just repackaged value/size.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Large-cap value tilt inside S&P 500: rank by book-to-market, earnings yield, cash-flow yield.
- Multi-factor scoring baseline: value + size + market beta controls.
- Factor-neutral portfolio construction.
Main risks:
- S&P 500 has limited size spread relative to all U.S. equities.
- Value can underperform for long periods.
- Accounting data timing matters.

06. Fama and French (2015)
Title: A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model
Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v116y2015i1p1-22.html
Core idea:
Adding profitability and investment factors improves the factor model. Firms with robust
profitability and conservative investment tend to have higher expected returns in the model.
Implementation translation:
- Features: operating_profitability, asset_growth, investment_rate, book_to_market.
- Use profitability and investment as quality/value companions.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long high profitability + conservative investment names.
- Avoid companies with aggressive asset growth and weak profitability.
- Multi-factor: value + profitability + conservative investment + momentum.
Main risks:
- Accounting definitions vary by vendor.
- Signal updates quarterly/annually; do not rebalance as if fundamentals update daily.

07. Asness, Moskowitz, and Pedersen (2013)
Title: Value and Momentum Everywhere
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1363476
Core idea:
Value and momentum both work across many markets and are often negatively correlated,
making them useful diversifiers. Combining them can improve portfolio stability.
Implementation translation:
- Build independent value and momentum scores.
- Combine scores instead of betting on only one factor.
- Consider dynamic factor weights based on drawdowns/regimes only after baseline is proven.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Composite score = z(value) + z(momentum), sector-neutral.
- Two-sleeve strategy: separate value and momentum portfolios, rebalance monthly.
- Long stocks ranked high on both value and momentum; avoid value traps with bad momentum.
Main risks:
- Combining factors can hide turnover and crowding.
- Value and momentum can both fail in certain regime transitions.

08. Piotroski (2000)
Title: Value Investing: The Use of Historical Financial Statement Information to Separate Winners from Losers
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=249455
Core idea:
Among value stocks, simple accounting strength signals can separate healthier value names
from value traps. The F-score uses profitability, leverage/liquidity, and operating efficiency.
Implementation translation:
- Build a 0-9 F-score using lagged financial statements.
- Apply it mainly within cheap/value stocks.
- Do not use it as a daily trading signal; update when new financial statements are available.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long high book-to-market stocks with F-score >= 7.
- Avoid or short low F-score value traps.
- Combine F-score with recent momentum to require both cheapness and improving perception.
Main risks:
- S&P 500 firms are larger and more covered than the original strongest anomaly universe.
- Financial data lags and restatements are critical.

09. Sloan (1996)
Title: Do Stock Prices Fully Reflect Information in Accruals and Cash Flows About Future Earnings?
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2598
Core idea:
Earnings backed by cash flows are more persistent than earnings driven by accruals.
Investors may overprice high-accrual firms and underprice low-accrual firms.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: accruals_to_assets = accounting accruals / total assets.
- Rank low accruals as better, high accruals as worse.
- Combine with profitability and quality.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long low-accrual profitable firms.
- Short high-accrual firms with weak cash conversion.
- Quality composite: gross profitability + low accruals + stable margins.
Main risks:
- Accrual definitions vary.
- Signal has weakened in some later samples and may be arbitraged/crowded.

10. Novy-Marx (2013)
Title: The Other Side of Value: Good Growth and the Gross Profitability Premium
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1598056
Core idea:
Gross profits scaled by assets is a simple and powerful predictor of stock returns.
Profitability complements value because profitable firms are often not the cheapest firms.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: gross_profitability = (revenue - cost_of_goods_sold) / total_assets.
- Rank high gross profitability as better.
- Combine with value to find reasonably priced quality.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long high gross-profitability names with reasonable valuation.
- Quality-value portfolio: high gross profitability + high earnings yield/free cash flow yield.
- Avoid firms with low gross profitability even if cheap.
Main risks:
- Industry differences are large; consider sector/industry neutralization.
- Needs clean, lagged fundamentals.

11. Asness, Frazzini, and Pedersen / related AQR research
Title: Quality Minus Junk
Source: https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/working-paper/quality-minus-junk
Core idea:
Quality can be modeled as profitability, growth, safety, and payout. High-quality stocks have
historically delivered attractive risk-adjusted returns relative to low-quality stocks.
Implementation translation:
- Features: profitability, earnings stability, balance-sheet safety, payout/shareholder yield,
  low leverage, low volatility, low beta, low accruals.
- Build a composite quality score.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long top-quality S&P 500 names, sector-neutral.
- Long quality-value: high quality but not expensive.
- Short "junk": low quality + expensive + negative momentum, if shorting is feasible.
Main risks:
- Quality is a broad concept; avoid overfitting too many sub-signals.
- Some quality definitions overlap with low volatility and profitability.

12. Frazzini and Pedersen (2014)
Title: Betting Against Beta
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w16601
Core idea:
Leverage-constrained investors may overpay for high-beta stocks and underpay for low-beta
stocks. A BAB-style factor is long low-beta assets and short high-beta assets, with leverage
used to balance market exposure.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: rolling_beta_to_spy, e.g., 1-year or 2-year daily regression beta.
- Rank low beta as attractive, high beta as unattractive.
- For long-only, tilt toward low beta and scale exposure if desired.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Low-beta defensive basket with volatility target.
- Long low-beta/high-quality stocks, short high-beta/low-quality stocks.
- Market exposure timing: reduce gross exposure when cross-sectional beta dispersion spikes.
Main risks:
- Pure BAB requires leverage and financing assumptions.
- Low-beta stocks can lag badly in speculative bull markets.

13. Moreira and Muir (2017)
Title: Volatility-Managed Portfolios
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208
Core idea:
Reducing exposure when realized volatility is high can improve Sharpe ratios because factor
volatility varies more than expected returns. This applies to market and factor portfolios.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: realized_volatility, e.g., trailing 21-day or 63-day daily realized volatility.
- Exposure multiplier roughly inverse to variance or volatility, with caps/floors.
- Apply to SPY exposure, momentum sleeve, value sleeve, or combined factor portfolio.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Vol-targeted SPY strategy.
- Vol-targeted long/short factor sleeves.
- Reduce exposure when realized volatility is above percentile threshold.
Main risks:
- Can whipsaw during volatility spikes and rebounds.
- Vol targeting increases turnover and may miss rebounds after crashes.

14. Barroso and Santa-Clara (2015)
Title: Momentum Has Its Moments
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2041429
Core idea:
Momentum has attractive returns but severe crash risk. Momentum volatility is time-varying
and predictable; scaling momentum by its recent risk can reduce crashes.
Implementation translation:
- Estimate recent volatility of the momentum portfolio or momentum factor.
- Scale momentum exposure down when momentum risk is high.
- Combine with market trend filters after baseline.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Risk-managed cross-sectional momentum.
- Momentum sleeve with volatility cap and drawdown circuit breaker.
- Long-only momentum strategy that moves partially to cash when momentum-vol is high.
Main risks:
- Volatility scaling parameter selection can be overfit.
- Requires a stable definition of the momentum sleeve return.

15. Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen (2012)
Title: Time Series Momentum
Source: https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf
Core idea:
Assets with positive own past returns tend to continue rising, and assets with negative own
past returns tend to continue falling. For S&P 500 work, this is most directly useful as a
market-level trend overlay.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: SPY/SPX 12-month excess return or price above moving average.
- If trend positive, allow equity exposure; if trend negative, reduce exposure or hedge.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- SPY trend-following overlay on top of stock-selection strategies.
- Market regime filter: only run high-beta longs when SPY 12-month trend is positive.
- Dynamic net exposure based on index trend.
Main risks:
- Trend filters can underperform in choppy sideways markets.
- Must specify cash return and tax assumptions if realistic.

16. Jegadeesh and Livnat (2006)
Title: Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift: The Role of Revenue Surprises
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=903767
Core idea:
Post-earnings drift is stronger when earnings surprise and revenue surprise point in the
same direction. Revenue-confirmed earnings beats are more credible than expense-only beats.
Implementation translation:
- Features: standardized earnings surprise, revenue surprise, announcement return.
- Enter after announcement, not before.
- Hold for 20-60 trading days or until next earnings event.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Long positive earnings surprise + positive revenue surprise.
- Short negative earnings surprise + negative revenue surprise.
- Filter by liquidity, analyst coverage, and gap size.
Main risks:
- Requires precise earnings announcement timestamps.
- Strategy may decay after costs and in highly covered large-cap stocks.

17. Amihud (2002)
Title: Illiquidity and Stock Returns: Cross-Section and Time-Series Effects
Source: https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/finance/docs/WP/2000/pdf/wpa00041.pdf
Core idea:
Illiquidity is related to expected returns. Less liquid stocks may earn compensation, but
liquidity also determines implementation costs and capacity.
Implementation translation:
- Feature: amihud_illiquidity = average(abs(return) / dollar_volume)
- In S&P 500, use liquidity mainly as a cost/capacity/risk filter.
- Avoid high-turnover strategies in names with poor dollar volume or wide spreads.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Liquidity-aware factor portfolios: penalize signals with high expected slippage.
- Capacity model: max position as fraction of ADV.
- Liquidity stress regime detector.
Main risks:
- S&P 500 is liquid overall, so cross-sectional return premium may be muted.
- Illiquidity can be a risk, not a free alpha source.

18. Gatev, Goetzmann, and Rouwenhorst (2006)
Title: Pairs Trading: Performance of a Relative-Value Arbitrage Rule
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1095996
Core idea:
Pairs trading buys one stock and shorts a historically similar stock when their price spread
diverges, betting on convergence.
Implementation translation:
- Pair formation: match stocks by normalized price history correlation/distance, ideally
  within sector/industry.
- Trading signal: z-score of spread vs rolling mean.
- Enter when spread z-score exceeds threshold; exit at mean or time stop.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Sector-constrained pairs trading among highly correlated S&P 500 stocks.
- Cluster-based relative value: pair each stock with industry basket.
- Long cheap spread leg, short rich spread leg, beta/sector neutral.
Main risks:
- Correlations break during regime shifts.
- Shorting, borrow, and transaction costs can erase returns.
- Price-distance pairs can be less stable than cointegration/factor residual methods.

19. Avellaneda and Lee (2010)
Title: Statistical Arbitrage in the U.S. Equities Market
Source: https://traders.berkeley.edu/papers/Statistical%20arbitrage%20in%20the%20US%20equities%20market.pdf
Core idea:
Model-driven statistical arbitrage can use PCA or ETF/factor regressions to isolate residual
returns, then trade mean reversion in residuals.
Implementation translation:
- Fit factor model: stock returns explained by market/sector ETFs or principal components.
- Compute residual return series and residual z-scores.
- Trade mean reversion in residuals while keeping factor exposure neutral.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- PCA residual mean-reversion on S&P 500 stocks.
- Sector ETF residual mean-reversion.
- Combine residual z-score with liquidity and short-term reversal filters.
Main risks:
- Crowding and factor crashes.
- Model instability and lookahead in PCA fitting.
- High turnover and cost sensitivity.

20. Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020)
Title: Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning
Source: https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276
Core idea:
Machine learning methods can improve return prediction when using many firm characteristics,
especially because relations are nonlinear and interactive. Tree-based models and neural
networks can outperform simple linear models in some settings.
Implementation translation:
- Build a point-in-time feature matrix with returns, fundamentals, momentum, volatility,
  liquidity, and interactions.
- Use walk-forward training only.
- Predict cross-sectional next-period returns or ranks, then form portfolios from predictions.
Useful S&P 500 algorithms:
- Gradient-boosted rank model for 1-month returns.
- ML ensemble using only pre-approved paper-derived factors.
- Meta-model that combines value, quality, momentum, volatility, and event features.
Main risks:
- Severe overfitting risk.
- Feature leakage is easy.
- Model interpretability and transaction costs must be monitored.

10 BATCHES OF ALGORITHMS TO DEVELOP
================================================================================

BATCH 1: Baseline Momentum and Trend
Primary papers:
- Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)
- Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen (2012)
- George and Hwang (2004)
Objective:
Create robust price-based baselines before touching fundamentals or ML.
Algorithms:
1. Cross-sectional 12-1 momentum: monthly rebalance, long top quintile, optional short bottom quintile.
2. 52-week-high momentum: monthly rebalance, long top quintile by price/high ratio.
3. SPY trend overlay: allow long exposure only when SPY 12-month trend is positive.
4. Momentum + trend filter: run cross-sectional momentum only when market trend is positive.
Implementation notes:
- Start long-only first to avoid borrow complications.
- Then test beta-neutral long/short.
- Compare equal weight vs inverse-vol weight.
Validation:
- Check turnover, crash periods, sector concentration, and beta.
- Require one-day execution lag.

BATCH 2: Smooth Momentum and Crash-Managed Momentum
Primary papers:
- Da, Gurun, and Warachka (2014)
- Barroso and Santa-Clara (2015)
- Moreira and Muir (2017)
Objective:
Improve the baseline momentum strategy's drawdown and crash behavior.
Algorithms:
1. Smooth momentum: high 12-1 momentum plus high fraction of positive days.
2. Jump-penalized momentum: penalize stocks where most momentum came from a few large daily returns.
3. Volatility-scaled momentum sleeve: reduce exposure when trailing momentum-portfolio vol is high.
4. Momentum with drawdown circuit breaker: reduce exposure after sleeve drawdown breach.
Implementation notes:
- Compute momentum sleeve daily returns to estimate sleeve volatility.
- Cap exposure scaling to avoid extreme leverage.
Validation:
- Focus on 2009 momentum crash, 2020 rebound, and 2022 drawdowns.
- Compare Sharpe and max drawdown to unscaled momentum.

BATCH 3: Value, Contrarian, and Reversal
Primary papers:
- Fama and French (1993)
- De Bondt and Thaler (1985)
- Asness, Moskowitz, and Pedersen (2013)
Objective:
Build value and contrarian baselines, then combine them with momentum to avoid value traps.
Algorithms:
1. Large-cap value: rank by book-to-market and earnings yield.
2. Long-term reversal: buy prior 3-year losers with improving 6-month momentum.
3. Value + momentum composite: z(value) + z(momentum).
4. Sector-neutral value: rank within sectors and allocate equal sector risk.
Implementation notes:
- Use point-in-time book equity and lag fundamentals.
- Treat negative book equity and financial-sector accounting carefully.
Validation:
- Watch 1999-2000, 2007-2009, 2017-2021 value drought, and 2022 value rebound.

BATCH 4: Quality and Fundamental Strength
Primary papers:
- Piotroski (2000)
- Novy-Marx (2013)
- Quality Minus Junk
- Sloan (1996)
Objective:
Create robust accounting-based quality strategies.
Algorithms:
1. Gross profitability rank: long high gross-profit/assets.
2. Low accruals quality: long low accruals, short high accruals if feasible.
3. Piotroski value quality: among cheap stocks, buy high F-score.
4. Composite quality: profitability + safety + low accruals + payout/shareholder yield.
Implementation notes:
- Quarterly/annual update cadence only.
- Use filing date plus lag if exact availability date is unknown.
- Sector-neutralize gross profitability because industry structures differ.
Validation:
- Compare quality-only, value-only, and quality-value composite.
- Track factor exposures to low beta and profitability.

BATCH 5: Profitability, Investment, and Multi-Factor Asset Pricing
Primary papers:
- Fama and French (2015)
- Hou/Xue/Zhang can be used later as an optional extension, but this batch is anchored on FF5.
- Novy-Marx (2013)
Objective:
Build an interpretable multi-factor model with profitability and investment discipline.
Algorithms:
1. Robust profitability + conservative investment long portfolio.
2. Avoid aggressive investment: underweight high asset-growth firms with weak profitability.
3. FF5 composite: value + profitability + conservative investment + low beta + momentum.
4. Factor-neutral alpha: residualize candidate scores against standard factor exposures.
Implementation notes:
- Asset growth and investment rate are slow-moving; monthly/quarterly rebalance is enough.
- Use standardized ranks by sector.
Validation:
- Check whether alpha survives controlling for value, momentum, beta, and sector.

BATCH 6: Low-Beta, Defensive, and Volatility-Managed Exposure
Primary papers:
- Frazzini and Pedersen (2014)
- Moreira and Muir (2017)
- Quality Minus Junk
Objective:
Build defensive strategies with lower drawdown and smoother exposure.
Algorithms:
1. Low-beta quality: long low-beta, high-quality names.
2. BAB-inspired long/short: long low-beta, short high-beta, beta-neutral if feasible.
3. Vol-managed SPY overlay: scale SPY exposure by trailing realized volatility.
4. Defensive factor blend: quality + low beta + low idiosyncratic volatility.
Implementation notes:
- Estimate beta with rolling daily returns; test 1-year and 2-year windows.
- Do not use unlimited leverage. Set exposure caps.
Validation:
- Compare lower drawdown vs lower CAGR tradeoff.
- Stress test speculative bull markets where low beta lags.

BATCH 7: Earnings Drift and Event Signals
Primary papers:
- Jegadeesh and Livnat (2006)
- Sloan (1996)
- Piotroski (2000)
Objective:
Build event-driven strategies around earnings announcements and post-announcement drift.
Algorithms:
1. Revenue-confirmed earnings drift: long positive EPS surprise + positive revenue surprise.
2. Negative drift: short/avoid negative EPS surprise + negative revenue surprise.
3. Earnings gap continuation: use announcement return as signal after event close.
4. Fundamental confirmation: only trade PEAD when quality/accruals agree with surprise.
Implementation notes:
- Earnings timestamps are essential. Do not trade before information was public.
- Use next open/close after announcement depending on timestamp.
Validation:
- Report pre-cost and post-cost returns.
- Split by mega-cap vs smaller S&P 500 names; PEAD may be weaker in mega-caps.

BATCH 8: Liquidity-Aware Short-Term Reversal and Execution-Constrained Alpha
Primary papers:
- Amihud (2002)
- Avellaneda and Lee (2010)
- Gatev, Goetzmann, and Rouwenhorst (2006)
Objective:
Build fast mean-reversion strategies while modeling liquidity and costs honestly.
Algorithms:
1. 1-week reversal among liquid S&P 500 names, sector-neutral.
2. Residual short-term reversal after removing market and sector returns.
3. Liquidity-filtered reversal: trade only names with low estimated slippage.
4. Turnover-constrained reversal: optimizer penalizes excessive turnover.
Implementation notes:
- This batch is cost-sensitive. Assume pessimistic slippage first.
- Use participation caps and skip illiquid names.
Validation:
- Run sensitivity table across slippage assumptions.
- Require positive results after costs, not just before costs.

BATCH 9: Relative Value, Pairs, and PCA Statistical Arbitrage
Primary papers:
- Gatev, Goetzmann, and Rouwenhorst (2006)
- Avellaneda and Lee (2010)
Objective:
Develop market/sector-neutral relative value strategies.
Algorithms:
1. Classic distance pairs: pair stocks within sectors by normalized price history distance.
2. Cointegration pairs: test stable pairs and trade z-score deviations.
3. Sector ETF residual mean reversion: regress each stock on SPY + sector ETF, trade residual z-score.
4. PCA residual mean reversion: fit PCA on rolling window, trade residual extremes.
Implementation notes:
- Fit pairs/PCA only using historical windows.
- Refit on schedule; do not use full-sample PCA.
- Enforce sector, beta, and dollar neutrality.
Validation:
- Monitor factor exposure, pair breakage, stop-loss frequency, and cost sensitivity.

BATCH 10: Machine Learning Meta-Model and Ensemble Portfolio
Primary papers:
- Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020)
- Asness, Moskowitz, and Pedersen (2013)
- Fama and French (2015)
Objective:
Use ML only after interpretable factors are implemented and tested.
Algorithms:
1. Gradient-boosted rank model using paper-derived features only.
2. Regularized linear model baseline: elastic net on standardized features.
3. ML ensemble: combine value, momentum, quality, risk, liquidity, and event features.
4. Meta-allocator: choose weights across already-built strategy sleeves using walk-forward data.
Implementation notes:
- Use expanding or rolling walk-forward training.
- Predictions should be ranks or expected returns for next month.
- Feature availability dates must be enforced.
- Log every model version, feature set, and parameter grid.
Validation:
- Compare ML against simple equal-weight factor ensemble.
- Require explainability: feature importance, monotonic sanity checks, and factor exposure report.
- Evaluate turnover and degradation under higher costs.
