# Batch 2: New Individual Strategies for Pool Expansion (H571–H580)

## Thesis
H559's lev-Sharpe SLSQP works on a 55-strategy pool. The pool composition is frozen at H543's
design. Two key insights suggest the pool can be improved:

1. **H265 emerged as a surprise 14.7% weight** in H559 despite near-zero weight in all prior MDR
   ensembles. Its low-autocorrelation fundamental signal pairs uniquely well with vol-targeting.
   Are there more strategies with this property?

2. **H191/H192 (PCA reversion, 12.9% combined)** entered H559 from a pool that includes them.
   Their daily-resolution mean-reversion is anti-correlated with vol spikes. Can we design more
   strategies with this "vol-spike hedge" property?

3. **The pool has zero QQQ/IWM strategies** (tech and small-cap AA). These assets add regime
   diversity the current SPY/TLT/GLD portfolio lacks.

Strategy design philosophy for this batch: build individual strategies, test them standalone
(keep bar: CI > 0, Sharpe > 0.51), then add the kept ones to the pool and run H559's lev-Sharpe
SLSQP on the expanded pool. The terminal strategy H580 is the expanded-pool ensemble.

## H571 — QQQ/TLT Risk Parity

**Signal:** H021-style risk parity but using QQQ (Nasdaq-100) and TLT only.
**Hypothesis:** QQQ has higher CAGR than SPY but deeper drawdowns. QQQ-TLT parity should produce
a strategy with higher CAGR than H021 (SPY-TLT) and similar or better Sharpe, because QQQ-TLT
correlation is deeply negative in tech crashes (2000-2002: QQQ -80%, TLT +30%).

**Data availability:** QQQ starts 1999-03-10. Folds 0-1 (1993-2000 train/1998-2000 test) will
have partial or zero data. Expected: 1-2 zero OOS folds at the start.

**Implementation:**
```python
class QQQTLTRiskParity(MultiAssetStrategy):
    ASSETS = ["QQQ", "TLT"]
    lookback: int = 63

    def generate_weights(self, prices, date):
        # same as H021 but for QQQ/TLT
        rets = prices[self.ASSETS].pct_change().dropna()
        if len(rets) < self.lookback:
            return {"QQQ": 0.5, "TLT": 0.5}
        recent = rets.tail(self.lookback)
        vols = recent.std() * sqrt(252)
        inv_vols = 1.0 / vols
        weights = inv_vols / inv_vols.sum()
        return dict(zip(self.ASSETS, weights))
```

**Success criteria:** Sh > 0.51, CI lower > 0, Max DD better than SPY (-55%).
**Expected H001 corr:** ~0.5 (QQQ more correlated with SPY than TLT, especially in rallies).

## H572 — IWM/TLT Risk Parity (Small-Cap Diversifier)

**Signal:** Risk parity using IWM (Russell 2000 small-cap) and TLT.
**Hypothesis:** IWM has lower correlation with TLT than QQQ has with TLT. Small-cap firms have
less duration exposure (less likely to be affected by rate movements than mega-cap tech). IWM-TLT
parity may produce a low-H001-corr strategy that fills a unique regime niche.

**Data availability:** IWM starts 2000-05-26. Folds 0-1 will be partial.

**Expected H001 corr:** Lower than H021 (~0.3-0.4) because IWM-TLT has different crisis behavior
than SPY-TLT (small-cap crashes harder than SPY but recovers independently).

**Success criteria:** H001 corr < 0.35 (valuable MDR pool addition) AND Sh > 0.51.

## H573 — QQQ/IWM/TLT Three-Asset Risk Parity

**Signal:** Risk parity over QQQ, IWM, TLT — the "growth + value + bonds" parity.
**Hypothesis:** Combining tech (QQQ) and small-cap (IWM) in a risk parity framework with TLT
creates a broader equity representation. During tech-heavy periods (2012-2019), QQQ dominates.
During value rotations (2000-2006, 2022), IWM and TLT dominate. TLT hedges both.

**Data availability:** Both QQQ (1999) and IWM (2000) start after 1993. Folds 0-2 will be partial.

**Risk:** 3 assets, two of which start late → likely 2 zero OOS folds. May fail keep bar.
Mitigation: fall back to SPY-only (H001) for folds with insufficient data.

**Success criteria:** Same as H572. The fold-0/1 zero-return issue is acceptable if folds 2-11
are all positive and Sharpe > 0.51.

## H574 — Low-Autocorrelation Fundamental (Earnings Surprise Momentum, Quarterly)

**Signal:** Cross-sectional: select top-quintile stocks by standardized unexpected earnings (SUE)
from quarterly earnings reports. Hold for exactly one quarter (90 days). Rebalance quarterly.
**Hypothesis:** H265's strong performance under lev-Sharpe was attributed to LOW AUTOCORRELATION
— quarterly fundamental signals have returns that are nearly uncorrelated with prior-quarter
returns. Post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD) is a well-documented anomaly. Quarterly
rebalance + fundamental signal = the exact profile the lev-Sharpe optimizer rewards.

**Signal construction:**
- Use quarterly earnings data from EDGAR (available from 2009)
- Compute SUE = (actual_EPS - expected_EPS) / std_error_of_forecast
- Rank by SUE within quarter, go long top quintile, equal-weight
- Rebalance on first trading day of each quarter following earnings release

**Data constraint:** EDGAR starts ~2009 → folds 0-5 zero. Only folds 6-11 contribute.

**Expected H001 corr:** Near zero or negative (earnings surprises are idiosyncratic, not
correlated with broad market). Should fall in the H265 cluster.

**Success criteria:** Sh > 0.51 in folds 6-11, CI > 0. Low H001 corr < 0.15.

## H575 — Daily Mean Reversion (H191-class, New Signal)

**Signal:** Daily mean reversion using cross-sectional residuals from a 3-factor model (market,
size, value). Short stocks with the highest 3-factor residual return yesterday; long stocks with
the lowest. Hold 1 day. Rebalance daily.
**Hypothesis:** H191/H192 (PCA reversion) earned 12.9% combined weight in H559 because their
daily-resolution reversal is anti-correlated with vol spikes. A 3-factor residual reversal
(rather than PCA residual) may be more stable out-of-sample because it uses economically
motivated factors rather than statistical ones that change as loadings shift.

**Key difference from H191:** H191 uses PCA to construct orthogonal factors; H575 uses
Fama-French-style market/size/value factors. The economic grounding should improve OOS stability.

**Signal construction:**
- Daily 3-factor regression using SPY (market), IWM-SPY (size), IVE-IVW (value) returns
- Stock residuals = actual_return - predicted_return from factors
- Long bottom decile (negative residual), short top decile (positive residual)
- Dollar-neutral, daily rebalance

**Data constraint:** Requires stock-level data. Check if existing infrastructure supports this.
If not, implement as ETF-level: long [IWM, XLP] when yesterday they underperformed SPY, short
[QQQ, XLU] when they outperformed. This is a coarser version but still tests the signal.

**Expected H001 corr:** Near -0.02 to +0.05 (market-neutral, anti-momentum).
**Success criteria:** Sh > 0.51 AND H001 corr < 0.10.

## H576 — Momentum + Quality Weekly Rotation (7-day hold)

**Signal:** Cross-sectional: weekly rebalance of top-20 S&P 500 stocks by combined momentum
(21-day return) AND quality (GP/A percentile, updated monthly). Equal-weight long only.
**Hypothesis:** H508 (semi-annual quality+momentum) earned 0.989 Sharpe with H001 corr near zero.
The "patience" frequency (semi-annual) was optimal. But weekly momentum + monthly quality update
might capture more frequent momentum signals while retaining the quality filter's stability.

**H508 lesson applied:** Quality filter reduces the universe before momentum selection. Momentum
runs in the quality-filtered universe. Do not weight quality and momentum equally — quality gates,
momentum ranks.

**Signal construction:**
- Monthly: mark top 40% of S&P 500 by GP/A as "quality universe" (e.g., 200 stocks)
- Weekly: within quality universe, select top 20 by 21-day return
- Rebalance weekly on Monday open

**Expected H001 corr:** 0.3-0.5 (momentum has meaningful H001 correlation; quality filter reduces
it somewhat).

**Success criteria:** Sh > 0.7 (higher bar for new strategies with data availability). Weekly
rebalance increases trade count dramatically — confirm realistic execution costs.

## H577 — Sector Momentum with Defensive Tilt

**Signal:** Monthly rebalance: rank all 11 SPDR sector ETFs by 12-1 momentum. Go long top 3,
short bottom 3 (if dollar-neutral improves) OR just long top 3. Apply vol-targeting at 12%
target (sector ETFs are more volatile than SPY).

**Data availability:** XLP (consumer staples), XLU (utilities), XLK (tech), XLV (health), XLF
(financials) etc. available from 1999. Most sectors available by fold 1.

**Hypothesis:** Sector momentum is a weaker version of factor rotation but has near-zero H001
corr in long-only form because it can concentrate in defensive sectors (XLP/XLU) for extended
periods when tech/financials have negative momentum.

**Differentiation from H063 (kept, Sharpe 0.577):** H063 uses VIX regime to switch between
SPY and defensive sectors. H577 uses pure momentum ranking — no regime conditioning.

**Success criteria:** Sh > 0.6, H001 corr < 0.5. Primary value: low-corr addition to MDR pool.

## H578 — VNQ/TLT Risk Parity (REITs as inflation hedge)

**Signal:** Risk parity using VNQ (US REITs) and TLT.
**Hypothesis:** REITs have a different return driver than SPY: real estate income (rent) rather
than earnings. VNQ-TLT correlation is typically low (-0.1 to +0.2) because REITs benefit from
falling rates (like TLT) but also benefit from rising real estate values (anti-TLT). This
ambiguous correlation structure may produce a stable risk parity portfolio.

**Data availability:** VNQ starts 2004-09-29. Folds 0-3 partial or zero.

**Expected H001 corr:** ~0.4 (REITs are equity-like in crashes but income-like in bull markets).
**Success criteria:** Sh > 0.51, H001 corr < 0.45. VNQ's 2008 crash (-74%) is a known risk.

available, the optimizer diversifies across them.

**Success criteria:** Sh_lev > H559 (1.879) on expanded pool. Improvement > 0.05 Sharpe is
strong evidence that new strategies are genuinely additive.

## H580 — QQQ/IWM Augmented H559 Pool (Tech + Small-Cap Expansion)

**Signal:** H559's lev-Sharpe SLSQP but the pool additionally includes:
- H571 QQQ/TLT parity
- H572 IWM/TLT parity
- H573 QQQ/IWM/TLT parity (if kept)
- Plus any other kept H571-H578 strategies

**Hypothesis:** The current MDR pool is SPY/TLT/GLD-dominated for AA. Adding QQQ/IWM-based AA
strategies brings tech-growth and small-cap exposure into the optimizer's menu. Under vol-
targeting, QQQ's higher vol would cause it to hold a lower leveraged weight, making it a
"dampened growth engine" component.

**Expected behavior:** In the 2012-2019 tech rally, QQQ strategies should earn leverage above 1×
when realized vol is low. In the 2020 COVID crash, QQQ strategies de-lever faster due to
higher absolute vol.

**Why this is the terminal Batch 2 strategy:** H580 directly tests whether expanding the AA
component of the pool improves lev-Sharpe ensemble performance. This is a clean experiment
because QQQ/IWM parity is structurally similar to SPY/TLT parity — we know the mechanism works,
we're testing whether adding these assets improves the optimization.

**Success criteria:** Sh_lev > H579 (the expanded general pool). If H580 > H579 > H559, the
QQQ/IWM components are specifically valuable. If H580 ≈ H579 > H559, the new components are
from H574-H578 rather than the AA additions.
