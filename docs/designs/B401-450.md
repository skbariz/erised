## Set 6 thesis

Set 6 theme: **"Event plus information architecture"** — corporate events, earnings expectations,
SEC filing text, conference-call tone, product-market competition, policy exposure, innovation and
patents, credit risk, ETF/passive ownership, and institutional ownership horizons.

Unlike Sets 1–5 (classic anomalies, factor models, volatility, macro, ML, governance, ESG, sentiment,
liquidity), Set 6 asks: *when is firm-specific information slow to reach prices?*

All signals are adapted to available data:
- Compustat fundamentals: gross_profit, total_assets, current_assets, current_liabilities,
  operating_cf, revenue, net_income, shares_outstanding, book_equity, long_term_debt
- Daily ETF/index prices: SPY, TLT, GLD, SHY, IEF, LQD, HYG, DBC, VNQ, IVE, IVW, IWM,
  QQQ, XLP, XLU, VIX
- No IBES earnings surprises, 13F filings, patent databases, credit ratings, or ETF holding data

### Data constraints (non-negotiable)
- No lookahead: fundamentals forward-filled from Compustat filing dates; use `.shift()` lag
- Sector-neutral: all CS strategies use `_sector_neutral_weights` with GICS sectors
- Quarterly rebalance default: 63-day period (lower turnover on slow fundamental signals)
- Keep bar: Sharpe > 0.51, 95% CI lower bound > 0

### Expected keep rate
40–45 / 50 (80–90%) — consistent with Sets 3–5.

### Terminal champion target
H450 MDR must beat H300/H350 (Sharpe 1.525) by incorporating Set 6 CS strategies as new diversifiers.

## Batch 1 — H401–H405: Earnings Quality & PEAD Proxies

**Source papers:** Bernard & Thomas (1989) PEAD; Skinner & Sloan (2002) Torpedo Risk; So (2013) Analyst Forecast Error

**Thesis:** Post-earnings-announcement drift and torpedo risk are driven by information quality and
valuation embedding. Without IBES data, we proxy with accruals (earnings quality), earnings momentum
(persistent positive surprise proxy), valuation × earnings trend (torpedo risk), revenue + NI
acceleration (beat-the-analyst proxy), and cash earnings persistence (signal durability).

**Regime expectations:**
- 2000–2002: low accruals / high quality avoids overstated tech earnings → large alpha
- 2008–2009: cash earnings superiority becomes visible; torpedo risk kills high-growth names
- 2020 COVID: accruals spike as cash flows collapse; high-quality persistent earners survive
- 2013–2017: signal moderate; low-vol era inflates expensive growth names (H403 headwind)

### H401 — AccrualQualityPort
**Signal:** Accruals = (NI − OCF) / avg_TA. Low accruals = high-quality cash earnings (Sloan 1996).
Long bottom 20% by accruals (lowest = best). Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** The accruals anomaly: market overvalues high-accrual earnings; corrects when cash reality hits.
This is a direct proxy for the Bernard & Thomas delayed-response story.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0, MaxDD < -40%.  
**Falsified if:** Signal reversed (high accruals earn more) or CI clips zero.

### H402 — EarningsMomentumQuality
**Signal:** Composite of (1) NI/TA YoY growth z-score + (2) OCF/TA YoY growth z-score.
Both growing simultaneously = confirmed quality earnings momentum. Long top 20%.  
**Why:** Proxy for consistent positive earnings surprises (PEAD). OCF confirmation filters
accrual-inflated earnings → higher quality signal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Adding OCF confirmation destroys versus pure NI growth.

### H403 — TorpedoRiskAvoid
**Signal:** Book/Market ratio (book_equity / market_cap) × NI growth sign. Long high-B/M + positive
NI growth; penalize low-B/M + declining NI (torpedo zone). Market cap = prices × shares_outstanding.  
**Why:** Skinner & Sloan: expensive growth stocks (low B/M) punished severely on negative earnings surprise.
We avoid that quadrant in a long-only portfolio.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Value (high B/M) provides no incremental value over quality alone.

### H404 — RevenueEarningsAcceleration
**Signal:** 2nd derivative: Δ(revenue YoY growth) + Δ(NI YoY growth) — acceleration of both signals.
Long top 20% accelerators. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** PEAD is strongest when the earnings beat was unexpected. Acceleration captures regime change
from poor to improving earnings — the setup for a positive surprise that hasn't been priced.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Acceleration adds nothing over level-based signals (H239, H402).

### H405 — CashEarningsPersistence
**Signal:** Rolling fraction of periods where OCF > 0 AND NI > 0 AND OCF/NI > 0.8 over 2 years.
High fraction = persistent cash earnings dominance. Long top 20%. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** So (2013): analyst forecast errors are predictable from historical earnings patterns. Persistent
cash-dominant earners have lower analyst error risk → less torpedo risk → better returns.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Persistence measure has too few qualifying stocks (>50% zero weights in folds).

## Batch 2 — H406–H410: Financial Statement Persistence & Disclosure Quality Proxies

**Source papers:** Li (2010) Forward-Looking Statements; Li (2008) Annual Report Readability

**Thesis:** SEC filing forward-looking language and readability correlate with earnings quality and
persistence. Without NLP access to 10-K text, we proxy with: earnings persistence (stable NI/TA),
cash flow reliability (OCF/NI consistency), book equity growth (retained earnings quality), balance
sheet improvement trend (proxy for positive disclosure trajectory), and working capital efficiency.

**Regime expectations:**
- 2000–2002: earnings persistence screens out bubble tech names with volatile NI
- 2008–2009: balance sheet strength (low debt + high OCF) dramatically outperforms
- 2020 COVID: working capital health signal: firms with WC/Rev > 0 survive better
- 2013–2017: weaker alpha period; balance sheet signal weakest in low-rate environment

### H406 — EarningsPersistencePort
**Signal:** Stability = 1 / rolling_std(NI/TA, 2 years). Low NI/TA variance = stable earnings.
Long most-stable top 20%. Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Li (2008) readability predicts earnings persistence. Stable earnings → more readable/reliable
disclosure → better future returns.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Stability merely selects mature/slow-growth defensives without return premium.

### H407 — CashFlowReliabilityPort
**Signal:** Rolling fraction of periods where OCF > NI over 2 years. High fraction = cash earnings
consistently dominate accruals → high reliability. Long top 20%. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Li (2010): forward-looking statements with higher positive tone predict more durable earnings.
Cash flow dominance is a balance-sheet proxy for that durability.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** OCF > NI frequency has no cross-sectional return predictability.

### H408 — BookValueGrowthPort
**Signal:** Book equity YoY growth = book_equity / book_equity.shift(252) − 1. Long top 20% growers.
Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Growing book equity = retained earnings quality → management confidence in future prospects
(proxy for positive forward-looking tone). Also: book value growth is a signal for financial flexibility.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Book value growth merely reflects share issuance, not retained earnings quality.

### H409 — BalanceSheetStrengthPort
**Signal:** Z-score composite of: (1) −(LTD/TA) change (debt declining is good), (2) OCF/TA change
(cash generation improving), (3) book_equity/TA change (equity building). Long top 20%.  
**Why:** Li (2010): forward-looking positive tone firms are those improving balance sheets. This
composite proxies for the trajectory of disclosure quality and financial flexibility.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Composite underperforms individual balance sheet metrics.

### H410 — WorkingCapitalHealthPort
**Signal:** Working capital ratio = (CA − CL) / Revenue. YoY change in this ratio = WC efficiency.
Long top 20% improving WC efficiency. Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Firms improving working capital efficiency are operationally disciplined → proxy for
forward-looking positive disclosure. Also: WC improvement predicts future cash flow conversion.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** WC/Revenue provides no incremental signal over OCF-based measures.

## Batch 3 — H411–H415: Sentiment & Tone Proxies via Fundamental Stability

**Source paper:** Mayew & Venkatachalam (2012) The Power of Voice

**Thesis:** Management vocal affect and tone during earnings calls contain incremental information.
Without transcript or audio access, we proxy with: consistent multi-quarter growth streaks (confident
management tone), fundamental-price divergence (misleading vs. authentic signals), gross margin
expansion (operational confidence), earnings variability (uncertainty proxy), and stable-margin
momentum (confirmed management credibility via sustained margins + price).

**Regime expectations:**
- 2000–2002: FundamentalDivergence signal very strong (prices up, fundamentals collapsing in tech)
- 2008–2009: Growth streaks break; MarginExpansion signal turns negative then reverses
- 2020 COVID: Streak signals reset; stable-margin names outperform volatile earners
- 2013–2017: Low-vol era; fundamental stability is less discriminating

### H411 — ConsistentGrowthStreak
**Signal:** Rolling fraction of trading days over 2 years where YoY revenue growth > 0 AND YoY NI growth > 0.
High fraction = persistent positive tone proxy. Long top 20%. Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Mayew & Venkatachalam: positive vocal affect predicts future firm performance. Consistently
growing companies (both top-line and bottom-line) proxy for management with persistently positive tone.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Streak signal is dominated by industry/sector membership, not firm-specific quality.

### H412 — FundamentalDivergenceAvoid
**Signal:** Divergence score = (NI/TA YoY growth z-score) − (6m price return z-score). Long positive
divergence (fundamentals outpacing price); underweight negative divergence (price running ahead of
fundamentals). Monthly rebalance to capture price dynamics.  
**Why:** Mayew & Venkatachalam: tone-price divergence detects when market under/overreacts.
Torpedo risk (Skinner & Sloan): prices up, fundamentals down → short-term reversion risk.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Fundamental-price divergence has no return predictability (efficient markets).

### H413 — MarginExpansionPort
**Signal:** GP/Revenue YoY change (margin expansion) with revenue growth > 0 filter. Long top 20%
margin expanders with confirmed revenue growth. Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Expanding margins with growing revenue = pricing power + competitive advantage + management
confidence (positive tone proxy). Mayew & Venkatachalam: confident management = better future performance.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** GP/Revenue margin change provides no incremental value over GP/TA level (H239).

### H414 — EarningsVariabilityAvoid
**Signal:** Rolling std of NI/TA over 2 years (low = stable). Long lowest-variance 20% (ascending=True).
Sector-neutral, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Mayew & Venkatachalam: uncertain management → higher vocal stress → weaker future performance.
High NI volatility = uncertain earnings environment = proxy for uncertain/stressed management tone.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** This is captured by H406 (EarningsPersistencePort) with high redundancy.

### H415 — StableMarginMomentum
**Signal:** Composite of (1) low GP/Revenue rolling std (stable margins) z-score + (2) 6m price
momentum z-score. Equal z-score blend. Long top 20%. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Combines Mayew proxy (stable margin = stable management) with momentum. Stocks with
confirmed price trend AND stable business model have double confirmation of positive trajectory.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0, H001 corr moderate (not >0.80).  
**Falsified if:** Adding stable margin does not improve over pure momentum baseline.

## Batch 4 — H416–H420: Product-Market & Competitive Position Proxies

**Source papers:** Hoberg & Phillips (2016) Text-Based Network Industries; Hoberg, Phillips & Prabhala (2014) Product Market Threats

**Thesis:** Without 10-K product description text or Hoberg-Phillips TNIC similarity data, we proxy
product-market signals with: persistent GP margin advantage (differentiation proxy), competitive
threat (margin compression + revenue deceleration), sector peer return momentum, defensive cash
hoarding, and market share proxy (revenue growth vs sector peers).

### H416 — DifferentiatedMarginPort
**Signal:** Long-run GP/Revenue level AND YoY stability. Composite z-score. Long top 20%.  
**Why:** Firms with persistently high, stable gross margins are differentiated; rivals haven't
competed away their pricing power. Proxy for low product-market fluidity.  

### H417 — CompetitivePressureAvoid
**Signal:** GP/Revenue YoY decline AND revenue growth deceleration simultaneously → competitive threat.
Filter: avoid bottom 20% of this composite; rank remaining by GP/TA quality.  
**Why:** High fluidity + margin compression + revenue slowdown = losing market position.  

### H418 — PeerGroupMomentum
**Signal:** Within-sector 6m return rank, filtered by positive NI growth. Sector leaders with
improving fundamentals. Long top 20% sector-relative performers with quality confirm.  
**Why:** Proxy for peer earnings spillover: within-sector momentum leaders likely receiving
positive competitive signals from peer announcements.  

### H419 — CashDefenseProxy
**Signal:** (CA/TA) YoY increase AND (GP/Revenue) declining → cash hoarding under competitive threat.
Long: NOT in this zone. Penalize cash hoarders with margin compression.  
**Why:** Product market fluidity triggers cash buildup + payout reduction. We want the opposite:
high margin + not hoarding cash = confident, differentiated firm.  

### H420 — MarketShareProxy
**Signal:** Revenue growth vs sector median. Long top 20% revenue outperformers within sector
(market share gainers). Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Peer-relative revenue growth is the best available proxy for market share gains
when product similarity text is unavailable.

## Batch 5 — H421–H425: Macro Sector & Policy Exposure Proxies

**Source paper:** Cohen, Diether & Malloy (2013) Legislating Stock Prices

**Thesis:** Without bill/vote data, policy exposure is proxied with sector ETF timing using
macro signals (yield curve, VIX, credit spreads, commodity prices). Regulated sectors
(utilities, financials, healthcare) are most sensitive.

### H421 — RegulatedSectorTiming
**Signal:** TLT + VIX regime gates on XLU/XLP sector allocation vs SPY. Rate-sensitive sectors go
defensive when TLT is rising and VIX is elevated.  
**Why:** Regulated utilities/consumer staples benefit from rate-falling regimes (legislative
tailwind from central bank policy); rate rises hurt them.  

### H422 — PolicyRateSectorRotate
**Signal:** SHY/TLT slope as yield curve proxy → rotate between rate-sensitive (XLU, XLP) and
growth (QQQ, IVW) sectors. ETF-based, weekly rebalance.  
**Why:** Policy rate changes (central bank, regulatory) most directly affect bond-like equity sectors.  

### H423 — CreditCycleSectorFilter
**Signal:** HYG/LQD spread as credit cycle proxy → allocate more to defensive sectors (XLP, XLU, IVE)
in widening credit spreads vs cyclicals in tightening spreads.  
**Why:** Legislative and regulatory risk often coincides with credit stress. Sector rotation on credit
regime is a proxy for policy risk exposure.  

### H424 — CommodityExposureTiming
**Signal:** DBC momentum overlay to time energy/materials sectors (via sector ETF proxies).
DBC trending up → overweight commodity-exposed sectors.  
**Why:** Commodity regulation and policy changes are reflected in DBC price before individual stocks.  

### H425 — MacroRegimeSectorBlend
**Signal:** Regime blender: VIX + TLT + HYG joint regime (3-signal) → rotate across XLP, XLU, QQQ,
IVE, IVW based on composite macro state. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Complex legislation creates macro regime changes that affect multiple sectors simultaneously.
The composite macro regime is a more robust policy signal than any single indicator.

## Batch 6 — H426–H430: Innovation & R&D Efficiency Proxies

**Source papers:** Hirshleifer, Hsu & Li (2013) Innovative Efficiency; Kogan et al. (2017) Patent Value; Eberhart et al. (2004) R&D Increases

**Thesis:** Without patent data or R&D expense (not in Compustat panel), we proxy innovation
efficiency with: asset turnover improvement (revenue productivity), knowledge capital (GP/TA
growth trajectory), growth reinvestment quality, tech-sector quality comparison, and operating
leverage improvement.

### H426 — AssetEfficiencyGrowth
**Signal:** Revenue/TA (asset turnover) YoY improvement. Long top 20% improvers. Quarterly.  
**Why:** Innovation efficiency = output per input. Revenue/TA improvement = more value per asset
unit = proxy for innovation productivity.  

### H427 — CapitalProductivityPort
**Signal:** GP/TA growth rate (knowledge capital productivity). Long top 20% GP/TA growers.  
**Why:** R&D-intensive firms convert knowledge capital to gross profit. GP/TA acceleration
proxies for increasing return on knowledge capital investment.  

### H428 — GrowthReinvestmentQuality
**Signal:** Book equity growth (retained earnings) + revenue growth + GP/TA level composite.
Reinvesting profitably while growing = quality growth signal.  
**Why:** Eberhart: unexpected R&D increases with balance-sheet strength predict long-term returns.
Proxy: book equity growing + revenue growing + high quality = disciplined reinvestment.  

### H429 — SectorInnovationProxy
**Signal:** QQQ vs IVE relative momentum (innovation vs value sector signal). Use as ETF-level
signal in the portfolio overlay framework (AA-style).  
**Why:** QQQ concentrates innovation leaders; IVE concentrates value/low-innovation. Their
relative strength signals which innovation regime is rewarding.  

### H430 — OperatingLeverageImprovement
**Signal:** GP/NI YoY improvement (GP expanding faster than NI). High GP but low NI = high
fixed cost structure (operating leverage building). Long top 20% GP/NI improvers.  
**Why:** R&D and innovation create operating leverage. Firms building operating leverage (high
GP/NI growth) are investing in scalable returns.

## Batch 7 — H431–H435: Credit Risk & Distance-to-Default

**Source papers:** Merton (1974) Credit Pricing; Vassalou & Xing (2004) Default Risk; Avramov et al. (2007) Momentum + Credit Rating; Kisgen (2006) Rating Thresholds

**Thesis:** Credit risk affects equity returns non-linearly. Distance-to-default, leverage
momentum, credit-filtered momentum, financial fragility avoidance, and credit-quality overlay
are all implementable from available fundamentals (book_equity, long_term_debt, total_assets,
net_income, operating_cf) + equity volatility from daily prices.

### H431 — MertonD2DPort
**Signal:** Simplified distance-to-default = book_equity / (book_equity + long_term_debt) as
financial health ratio, combined with equity volatility (rolling 63-day return std). High D2D
proxy = high book equity fraction + low realized vol. Long top 20%.  
**Why:** Merton (1974): equity is a call option; D2D measures distance from default strike.
Long high-D2D names avoids credit-stressed equities.  

### H432 — LeverageAdjustedMomentum
**Signal:** 6m price momentum filtered by financial leverage (LTD/TA). Long top 20% momentum
names with LTD/TA in bottom half. Avoid leveraged momentum names.  
**Why:** Avramov et al.: momentum profits are larger in high-quality/low-credit-risk firms.
Conditioning momentum on leverage removes the distress tail risk.  

### H433 — CreditHealthQuality
**Signal:** Composite: GP/TA quality rank + low LTD/TA rank + positive OCF/NI rank.
Credit-healthy quality portfolio. Long top 20%.  
**Why:** Vassalou & Xing: default risk must be handled with controls. This composite ensures
quality firms are also credit healthy — avoids quality value traps with hidden leverage.  

### H434 — FinancialFragilityAvoid
**Signal:** Altman Z-score proxy from available data: Z ≈ 1.2*(CA-CL)/TA + 1.4*(NI/TA
trailing) + 3.3*(EBIT_proxy/TA) + 0.6*(book_equity/LTD) + 1.0*(Revenue/TA).
Long top 20% by Z-proxy (most financially sound).  
**Why:** Kisgen (2006): firms near rating thresholds behave differently; Altman Z separates
fragile from sound firms. Z-proxy from Compustat captures the same financial health dimension.  

### H435 — CreditConditionedQualityPort
**Signal:** GP/TA quality (like H239) but restricted to membership with LTD/TA < sector median
AND OCF > 0. Credit-healthy subsample of the quality universe.  
**Why:** Credit condition as membership filter rather than signal. Ensures quality firms
are also credit healthy — avoids sector-specific distorted quality scores in leveraged names.

## Batch 8 — H436–H440: ETF Crowding & Passive Ownership Proxies

**Source papers:** Ben-David et al. (2018) ETF Volatility; Israeli et al. (2017) ETF Dark Side

**Thesis:** Without ETF holdings or flow data, ETF crowding is proxied by: market-cap
concentration within sectors (passive ownership proxy via index weight), sector ETF divergence
from equal-weight (flow signal), inverse-index-weight momentum (less-covered names get stronger
fundamental signals), within-sector correlation (ETF arbitrage proxy), and low-index-beta quality.

### H436 — MarketCapConcentrationAvoid
**Signal:** Market cap rank within sector (price × shares). UNDERWEIGHT top 5% by market cap
in each sector (mega-cap = most passive-owned). Long sector-neutral quality in mid-cap names.  
**Why:** Ben-David: high ETF ownership → higher vol, lower firm-specific information. Mid-cap
S&P 500 names have more information advantage.  

### H437 — SectorETFDivergence
**Signal:** When equal-weighted sector return (from constituent panel) diverges from market-cap-
weighted (approximated by sector ETF prices), trade mean reversion back toward the small-cap
within-sector names. ETF-level AA strategy using XLP, XLU, IVE, IVW, QQQ.  
**Why:** Israeli et al.: ETF-driven moves create non-fundamental dislocations. Divergence
between equal-weight and cap-weight is a proxy for ETF flow pressure.  

### H438 — InverseIndexWeightMomentum
**Signal:** 6m momentum signal, with position size inversely proportional to market cap rank
within sector. Smaller S&P 500 names get larger positions. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Less-passive-owned (smaller) S&P 500 names should have stronger momentum because
price discovery is less ETF-dominated → signals persist longer.  

### H439 — SectorCorrelationBreak
**Signal:** Rolling within-sector correlation (past 63 days). When correlation spikes (ETF flow
dominates), shrink positions. When correlation normalizes, restore positions. Applied as a
position-sizing overlay on GP quality.  
**Why:** Israeli et al.: high return synchronicity = ETF crowding = reduced idiosyncratic signal.
Lower weights when cross-stock correlation is elevated.  

### H440 — LowIndexBetaQuality
**Signal:** GP/TA quality rank, with priority to stocks with low beta to SPY (rolling 252-day
beta). Low-beta quality names are less passive-crowded.  
**Why:** Ben-David: ETF-heavy names have higher index beta. Low-beta quality = less ETF
ownership → stronger fundamentals-to-price link → better expected alpha.

## Batch 9 — H441–H445: Institutional Ownership Horizon Proxies

**Source papers:** Bushee (1998) Institutional Investors & R&D; Yan & Zhang (2009) Institutional Investors & Equity Returns

**Thesis:** Without 13F filing data, institutional horizon is proxied by: shareholder alignment
(low dilution + high OCF), transient-ownership avoidance (high revenue volatility + share
issuance), dedicated-owner proxy (consistent buyback + quality), smart accumulation (price > MA
+ improving fundamentals), and owner-mix quality composite.

### H441 — ShareholderAlignedPort
**Signal:** Low dilution (shares_outstanding growth ≤ 0) + high OCF/TA + positive 6m momentum.
All three conditions required for selection. Long qualifying top 20%.  
**Why:** Dedicated long-horizon institutions support low-dilution, high-cash-flow firms. This
triple condition mimics their selection criteria.  

### H442 — TransientOwnershipAvoid
**Signal:** Avoid stocks with high revenue volatility (rolling std/mean of revenue YoY growth)
AND share issuance (shares growth > 0). Long the remainder, ranked by GP quality.  
**Why:** Bushee: transient institutions pressure management for short-term EPS, leading to
R&D cuts and revenue volatility. Avoiding these avoids the Bushee transient ownership trap.  

### H443 — DedicatedOwnerProxy
**Signal:** Consistent share buyback (shares_outstanding YoY decline) + positive NI/TA trend + high
GP/TA level. Long top 20% of this composite. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Firms with consistent buybacks + quality = preferred by dedicated long-horizon holders.
Yan & Zhang: these firms predict better future earnings surprises.  

### H444 — SmartAccumulationProxy
**Signal:** Price > 200-day MA AND NI/TA improving YoY AND GP/TA improving YoY. All-conditions
filter, then rank by composite improvement score. Long qualifying names.  
**Why:** Yan & Zhang: short-term institutional accumulation signals predict future earnings
surprises. Price above MA + improving fundamentals = proxy for smart accumulation.  

### H445 — OwnerMixQualityPort
**Signal:** Composite of: (1) low shares growth z-score, (2) OCF reliability z-score (H407 signal),
(3) book value growth z-score (H408 signal). Equal z-score blend. Long top 20%.  
**Why:** All three signals proxy for institutional owner mix weighted toward long-horizon holders
vs transient institutions. The composite is more robust than any single proxy.

## Implementation notes

### Recommended build order
1. H401–H405 first (Batch 1): highest-ROI earnings quality signals, simplest data contracts
2. H431–H435 (Batch 7): credit risk from book fundamentals + price vol — genuinely new dimension
3. H406–H410 (Batch 2): persistence/reliability signals — incremental over Batch 1
4. H441–H445 (Batch 9): institutional proxies — need share dilution signals carefully
5. H411–H415 (Batch 3): tone/sentiment proxies — complex; implement after simpler batches validate
6. H436–H440 (Batch 8): ETF crowding — market cap computations require care
7. H416–H420 (Batch 4): competitive position — sector-relative signals, moderate complexity
8. H421–H425 (Batch 5): macro sector rotation — AA strategies, clean implementation
9. H426–H430 (Batch 6): innovation proxies — require asset efficiency calculations
10. H446–H450 (Batch 10): ensemble only after constituent batches produce results

### Key implementation rules
- Every CS strategy: sector-neutral via `_sector_neutral_weights`, quarterly rebalance (63-day default)
- Every AA strategy: `walk_forward_multi` if MultiAssetStrategy subclass; `walk_forward` otherwise
- Market cap = `prices_panel * _align_fund(shares_outstanding, prices_panel)` — use 63d lag for safety
- Accruals lag: fundamental data is already forward-filled from Compustat; additional shift not needed
- Revenue GP ratio: only defined where Revenue > 0; clip to [0, 2] to handle negative revenue edge cases
- Book equity / LTD: clip at 0 to avoid negative book equity distorting ratio rankings

### What falsification looks like
- CI clips zero in more than 3 / 5 batches → Set 6 thesis rejected for this data regime
- H450 MDR does not beat H300/H350 → Set 6 CS strategies fail to add MDR diversification
