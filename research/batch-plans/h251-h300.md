# H251–H300 Development Plan
**Source:** sp500_quant_paper_learnings_set3_for_10_algo_batches.txt  
**Scope:** 10 batches × 5 hypotheses = 50 strategies  
**Framework:** Walk-forward OOS 1993–2022, 5yr train / 2yr test, 12 folds, 1.5 bps all-in cost  
**Keep bar:** CI lower bound > 0, OOS Sharpe > 0.51 (SPY benchmark)

---

## Data availability reality check

Available in current infrastructure:
- Daily OHLCV for S&P 500 individual constituents ✓
- Quarterly fundamentals (balance sheet, income statement, cash flow) ✓
- Point-in-time S&P 500 membership ✓

- Analyst coverage counts (no vendor data)

economic mechanism.

## Batch 1 — H251–H255: Idiosyncratic Volatility & Lottery Risk Avoidance
**Source papers:** Ang et al. (2006), Bali et al. (2011)  
**Theme:** Stocks with high idiosyncratic vol or extreme lottery-like single-day returns earn
anomalously low future returns. Build the avoid layer before overlaying any other signal.

### H251 — IdiosyncraticVolFactor
**Signal:** Residual volatility from rolling 63-day OLS regression of stock return on SPY return.
Rank cross-sectionally; long bottom quintile (low idio vol), equal-weight.

**Why it works:** High idio vol is associated with lottery demand, distress, and low institutional
ownership. Unlike total vol (H176), residual vol strips out systematic risk, isolating the
stock-specific noise premium.

**Distinction from H176 (Low Volatility Anomaly):** H176 uses 12-month total realized vol.
H251 uses 63-day residual vol after removing market beta — captures idiosyncratic risk, not
market exposure.

**Expected regime behavior:**
- 2000–2002: Strong — high idio vol tech names punished
- 2008–2009: Moderate — distress spikes idio vol; works but may lag
- 2013–2017: Weaker — low-vol environment compresses spread
- 2020: Sharp drawdown possible during March liquidity shock

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.70, Max DD < −45%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If residual vol adds nothing beyond total vol (H176 corr > 0.90)

---

### H252 — MaxReturnLotteryAvoid
**Signal:** Maximum single daily return over the past 21 trading days (MAX21). Rank ascending;
long bottom quintile (low MAX), equal-weight.

**Why it works:** MAX captures lottery-preference demand. Retail investors overpay for extreme
upside optionality, depressing subsequent returns for high-MAX stocks.

**Distinction from H197 (Low Skewness Anomaly):** H197 uses distributional skewness over 252
days. H252 uses the single peak daily return over 21 days — a more event-driven, shorter-window
signal targeting lottery spikes specifically.

**Expected regime behavior:**
- 2000–2002: Strong — lottery demand peaked pre-crash
- 2008–2009: Mixed — crisis shocks inflate MAX across quality and distress names alike
- 2020: Difficult — COVID-driven spikes are systematic, not lottery-specific

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.65, Max DD < −50%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If MAX21 is merely a lagged volatility proxy with no incremental IC over H251

---

### H253 — DownsideBetaFactor
**Signal:** Downside beta: OLS slope of stock excess return on SPY excess return using only
observations where SPY return < 0, rolling 252 days. Long lowest-quintile downside-beta stocks.

**Why it works:** Downside beta measures crash sensitivity better than unconditional beta.
Investors dislike assets that lose most when the market falls — low downside-beta stocks offer
asymmetric protection.

**Distinction from H175 (BettingAgainstBeta):** H175 uses unconditional beta across all days.
H253 uses only down-market days to estimate crash sensitivity — distinct signal, lower correlation.

**Expected regime behavior:**
- 2008–2009: Strong — high downside-beta names punished severely
- 2013–2017: Weak — low-volatility era compresses downside-beta premium
- 2020: Strong March spike benefit

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.70, Max DD < −45%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If downside beta is empirically indistinguishable from total beta in S&P 500 sample

---

### H254 — TailRiskComposite
**Signal:** Equal-weight z-score composite of H251 (idio vol), H252 (MAX21), H253 (downside
beta), and 21-day rolling negative skewness. Rank ascending; long bottom quintile.

**Why it works:** Each tail-risk component captures a slightly different aspect of lottery/crash
risk. Combining reduces measurement noise and single-metric sensitivity.

**Expected regime behavior:** Should be more stable than any single component. Weaker in
low-vol regimes, stronger in high-vol or dispersion-rich regimes.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.80, Max DD < −42%, CI lower > 0, better than H253 standalone  
**Falsification:** If composite Sharpe is materially below best individual component (dilution)

---

### H255 — TailFilteredMomentum
**Signal:** Start with H157-style vol-scaled cross-sectional momentum (12-1 month). Apply
tail-risk screen: exclude top-decile idio-vol AND top-decile MAX21 stocks before ranking.
Equal-weight remaining top-quintile momentum names.

**Why it works:** Momentum in high-idio-vol / high-MAX stocks is often lottery noise, not
information. Removing these stocks from the momentum universe improves signal quality.

**Expected regime behavior:** Should reduce momentum crash severity (avoids high-vol names
during crashes) while preserving momentum continuation in fundamentally sound movers.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 1.00, Max DD < −30%, higher Sharpe than H157 (1.079)  
**Falsification:** If removing lottery names does not improve Sharpe or reduces it (signal was
in the high-idio-vol names)

## Batch 2 — H256–H260: Distress Avoidance & Value-Trap Filter
**Source papers:** Ohlson (1980), Campbell, Hilscher & Szilagyi (2008)  
**Theme:** Financially distressed stocks earn anomalously low returns. Screen them out of value,
quality, and profitability strategies before selecting.

### H256 — OhlsonOScoreDistress
**Signal:** Proxy Ohlson O-score from quarterly fundamentals:
components = leverage (liabilities/assets), working capital/assets, current ratio,
net income/assets, cash flow from operations/liabilities, size-adjusted total assets.
Standardize and combine. Rank ascending; long bottom-quintile (low distress).

**Why it works:** Accounting-based distress predicts low returns in addition to market-based
distress signals. The accounting signal is slow-moving and distinct from price momentum.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.65, Max DD < −45%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If O-score proxy is too highly correlated with existing quality factors (H168,
H208) — check cross-factor IC before finalizing

---

### H257 — CHSMarketDistress
**Signal:** CHS-style distress composite using market equity (size), profitability, recent
3-month return, 12-month volatility, and leverage. Proxy failure probability via logistic
function of standardized inputs with published-sign constraints. Long low-distress quintile.

**Why it works:** Market-based inputs (returns, vol, equity) react faster than accounting data.
The CHS combination of both market and accounting inputs is more predictive than either alone.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.70, Max DD < −45%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If H257 and H256 are correlated above 0.85 (not additive)

---

### H258 — DistressTrendFactor
**Signal:** QoQ change in combined distress score (delta of H256 proxy). Rank ascending; long
improving distress (score falling), avoid worsening distress.

**Why it works:** A rising distress score predicts further deterioration. Trend in balance-sheet
health is informative beyond level — similar to the level-vs-change dynamic found in H201–H250.

**Note from program history:** Level typically beats change in this codebase (feedback_level_vs_change_signals).
This is an explicit test of whether distress is an exception.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.60, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If change signal underperforms level signal (H256) as expected from prior program findings

---

### H259 — DistressFilteredValue
**Signal:** Value score (book-to-market or earnings yield) intersected with low distress screen.
Only include stocks in top-quintile value AND bottom-half CHS distress score. Equal-weight.

**Why it works:** Cheap stocks can be value traps. The CHS filter removes cheap-but-dangerous
names, leaving only fundamentally cheap stocks with manageable distress risk.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.75, Max DD < −45%, CI lower > 0, Sharpe > raw value factor  
**Falsification:** If distress filter excludes so many value names that N < 20 per rebalance

---

### H260 — LowDistressQualityMomentum
**Signal:** Three-way screen: top-third gross profitability (H227 signal), top-half 12-1
momentum, AND bottom-half CHS distress score. Equal-weight surviving names.

**Why it works:** Combines the strongest standalone signal family (quality/profitability) with
momentum confirmation and a distress guard to prevent late-cycle blowups.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.90, Max DD < −40%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If the intersection leaves N < 15 names per rebalance

## Batch 3 — H261–H265: Accounting Sustainability
**Source papers:** Hirshleifer et al. (2004), Titman et al. (2004), Richardson et al. (2005)  
**Theme:** Accounting quality signals beyond profitability — balance-sheet bloat, unreliable
accruals, and aggressive capital spending all predict lower returns.

### H261 — LowNetOperatingAssets
**Signal:** Net Operating Assets = (operating assets − operating liabilities) / total assets.
Approximation: NOA = (total assets − cash − investments) − (total liabilities − short-term debt
− long-term debt) / total assets. Rank ascending; long bottom-quintile.

**Why it works:** High NOA reflects cumulative accounting earnings that exceeded cumulative cash
generation. Investors overpay for balance-sheet bloat mistaking it for asset growth.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.70, Max DD < −45%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If highly correlated with existing accrual factors (H166 Low Accruals Quality)

---

### H262 — AccrualReliabilityFactor
**Signal:** Total accruals = net income − operating cash flow, scaled by average assets.
Decompose into working capital accruals (current assets/liabilities changes) and noncurrent
accruals (depreciation, long-term items). Long low total-accrual quintile.

**Why it works:** Less reliable accruals are less persistent. Investors overprice accrual-heavy
earnings, leading to predictable future underperformance as reality catches up.

**Distinction from H166 (Low Accruals Quality):** H166 used a simpler accrual proxy.
H262 uses the Richardson et al. decomposition: total accruals from cash flow statement directly.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.72, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If correlation with H166 exceeds 0.90

---

### H263 — ConservativeCapexInvestment
**Signal:** YoY change in capex-to-assets, sector-normalized. Rank ascending; long bottom-quintile
(low capex growth). Require sector normalization — capital-intensive sectors treated separately.

**Why it works:** Aggressive investment growth signals empire-building without profit discipline.
The cross-sectional pattern holds even controlling for sector capital intensity.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.68, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If capex growth is merely a proxy for existing asset growth (H289)

---

### H264 — SustainableEarningsComposite
**Signal:** Equal-weight composite of: (1) low NOA (H261), (2) low total accruals (H262),
(3) high operating cash flow / net income ratio (cash earnings purity). Sector-normalize each.

**Why it works:** Three independent angles on whether earnings are cash-backed and sustainable.
Combining reduces noise from any single accounting approximation.

**Note from program history:** Max 2–3 factor composites before dilution kicks in. This is a
3-factor composite — deliberate test of the dilution boundary.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.82, beats H261 and H262 standalone, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If composite Sharpe falls below H261 (dilution confirmed at 3 factors)

---

### H265 — SustainabilityFilteredProfitability
**Signal:** Gross profitability (H227 signal) filtered by H264 sustainability composite. Require
top-half sustainability score before including a high-profitability name. Equal-weight.

**Why it works:** Gross profitability (GP/assets) is durable but can include firms with
aggressive revenue recognition. The sustainability filter ensures the GP is cash-backed.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.92, Max DD < −38%, beats H227 standalone (0.878)  
**Falsification:** If filter reduces N below 20 per rebalance or if GP correlation with sustainability
is already so high that the filter adds nothing

## Batch 4 — H266–H270: R&D and Intangible-Aware Value
**Source papers:** Chan, Lakonishok & Sougiannis (2001)  
**Theme:** Accounting book value understates firms that invest heavily in R&D because R&D is
expensed. Adjust value and quality signals for R&D intensity.

### H266 — RDIntensityFactor
**Signal:** R&D expense / total assets, sector-normalized. Long top-quintile (high R&D intensity)
in tech, healthcare, and communications sectors only. Missing R&D treated as zero.

**Why it works:** Markets may underprice firms with high hidden intangible assets, especially
when paired with reasonable valuations and non-distressed balance sheets.

**Scope note:** Only meaningful in R&D-intensive sectors. Running on full S&P 500 dilutes signal.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.65, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If restricted-sector version underperforms full-sample gross profitability

---

### H267 — RDToMarketValueFactor
**Signal:** R&D expense / market equity, as a value-like signal. High R&D-to-market suggests the
market is not fully crediting intangible investment. Long top-quintile, sector-adjusted.

**Why it works:** Treats R&D spend as an asset. Stocks with high R&D/price are cheap on
intangible-adjusted terms even if expensive on traditional book-to-market.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.68, CI lower > 0, incremental vs H267 (non-overlapping IC)  
**Falsification:** If R&D/market equity is merely a growth-stock proxy with no incremental predictive power

---

### H268 — IntangibleAdjustedBTM
**Signal:** Capitalize R&D using perpetual inventory method (cumulative R&D × amortization
factor added back to book equity). Compute adjusted book-to-market. Long top-quintile.

**Why it works:** Traditional B/M penalizes R&D-intensive firms as expensive. Adjusting B/M
for capitalized R&D reveals genuinely cheap intangible-asset-rich firms.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.70, CI lower > 0, Sharpe > H226 (raw B/M momentum, 0.766)  
**Falsification:** If intangible-adjusted B/M is not materially different from raw B/M after sector normalization

---

### H269 — RDRecoverySignal
**Signal:** Three-way screen: high R&D intensity (top half, sector-adjusted) AND poor past 12
month return (bottom third) AND low CHS distress score (bottom half). Equal-weight survivors.

**Why it works:** High-R&D beaten-down stocks are often oversold. If distress is low, the
selloff reflects sentiment not fundamental deterioration — a recovery setup.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.72, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If N is consistently < 10 per rebalance (three-way screen too restrictive)

---

### H270 — IntangibleQualityComposite
**Signal:** Composite of R&D intensity (H266), operating profitability (H171 signal), and
low capex growth (H263) — equally weighted, restricted to R&D-intensive sectors.

**Why it works:** R&D-intensive quality: firms that invest heavily in intangibles, convert those
investments into profits, and do not simultaneously over-expand tangible assets.

**Note:** Sector-restricted composite will have lower diversification than full-S&P composites.
Expect wider CI.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.75, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If Sharpe < H171 operating profitability in same sector subset

## Batch 5 — H271–H275: Volume State and Momentum Life Cycle
**Source papers:** Lee & Swaminathan (2000), Gervais et al. (2001), Barber & Odean (2008)  
**Theme:** Volume and attention state signal where a stock sits in its momentum life cycle.
Early momentum (low turnover) continues; crowded momentum (extreme turnover) reverses.

### H271 — EarlyStageMomentum
**Signal:** 12-1 month momentum rank, conditioned on turnover state. Weight momentum by
(1 − turnover_rank), so high-momentum / low-turnover stocks score highest. Equal-weight top quintile.

**Why it works:** High-momentum / high-turnover stocks are crowded; their price
already reflects widespread knowledge. Low-turnover momentum names represent earlier diffusion.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.95, Max DD < −35%, beats H157 vol-scaled momentum (1.079)  
**Falsification:** If turnover rank has near-zero IC with momentum continuation in OOS folds

---

### H272 — HighVolumeReturnPremium
**Signal:** Flag stocks with abnormal volume (current 5-day average dollar volume > 2× 252-day
average) AND positive 5-day return context (return > 0). Long top-quintile flagged stocks for 21
days. Rebalance monthly.

**Why it works:** Visibility theory: abnormal volume increases investor recognition, driving
further demand. The non-distress version of a high-volume event is a genuine attention-driven premium.

**Caveats:** Earnings-day volume excluded (within ±3 days of announcement). Crisis-period volume
spikes filtered by VIX > 35 regime flag.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.75, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If the premium disappears after removing earnings-adjacent events

---

### H273 — VolumeConfirmedBreakout
**Signal:** 63-day price high breakout (close > 63-day rolling max, excluding today) combined
with abnormal volume (5-day dollar volume > 1.5× 252-day average). Long qualifying stocks.

**Why it works:** Price breakouts without volume confirmation are often false; breakouts with
volume confirmation signal real institutional sponsorship. Related to 52-week high (H195) but adds
the volume gate.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.78, CI lower > 0, H195 corr < 0.70  
**Falsification:** If volume gate eliminates too many breakout signals, leaving N < 15 per rebalance

---

### H274 — AttentionReversalOverlay
**Signal:** Flag high-attention overpriced names: top-decile MAX21 (H252) AND top-decile
21-day abnormal volume AND bottom-half gross profitability. **Underweight** these in any
long-only portfolio, or build as a standalone short-avoidance screen.

**Why it works:** Attention-grabbing stocks without fundamental support attract retail buying,
creating temporary overpricing that mean-reverts. The three-way filter (MAX + volume + weak
fundamentals) isolates genuine lottery froth.

**Note:** May be most valuable as an overlay on existing strategies rather than standalone.
Test standalone first to establish baseline.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.65 standalone, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If MAX and volume alone explain all predictive power (profitability filter adds nothing)

---

### H275 — VolumeMomentumLifeCycle
**Signal:** Composite momentum-volume state score. For each stock compute:
(1) 12-1 momentum percentile, (2) 21-day turnover percentile, (3) MAX21 percentile.
Score = momentum_pct − 0.5 × turnover_pct − 0.3 × MAX_pct. Long top-quintile.

**Why it works:** Rewards stocks with strong momentum that are not yet crowded and do not exhibit
lottery spike characteristics. The formula is the momentum-lifecycle hypothesis operationalized.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 1.00, Max DD < −32%, beats H157 (1.079)  
**Falsification:** If the penalty terms (turnover, MAX) reduce Sharpe vs. raw momentum

## Batch 6 — H276–H280: Skewness, Coskewness and Crash-Aware Portfolio
**Source papers:** Harvey & Siddique (2000)  
**Theme:** Investors pay a premium to hold assets that improve (rather than worsen) portfolio
skewness during bad markets. Price this in cross-sectional rankings.

### H276 — NegativeSkewnessAvoidance
**Signal:** 252-day rolling skewness of daily returns. Rank ascending; long bottom-quintile
(most positive skew, i.e., right-tailed returns). Sector-normalize.

**Note from program history:** H197 (Low Skewness Anomaly) used the same general mechanism.
Check if they produce different results: H197 used negative of skewness as signal. This is
an explicit replication check with sector-neutral treatment.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.85, H197 corr < 0.75, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If results are statistically indistinguishable from H197 (confirmed duplicate)

---

### H277 — CoskewnessPortfolioPenalty
**Signal:** Rolling 252-day coskewness with SPY: E[(r_i − μ_i)(r_m − μ_m)²] normalized by
vol estimates. Rank ascending; long low-coskewness (assets that do NOT worsen market tail risk).

**Why it works:** Coskewness captures whether a stock loses more when the market has extreme
bad days. Stocks with high coskewness with SPY are disproportionately painful to hold.

**Distinction from H253 (Downside Beta):** Downside beta captures mean of down-market excess
returns. Coskewness captures interaction with squared market moves — a higher-moment measure.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.72, H253 corr < 0.80, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If coskewness is empirically redundant with downside beta in S&P 500 universe

---

### H278 — LeftTailLossFactor
**Signal:** Rolling 252-day CVaR at 5% level (average of worst 5% of daily returns). Rank
ascending; long names with smallest (least negative) CVaR. Sector-normalize.

**Why it works:** CVaR is a more complete measure of left-tail risk than standard deviation or
beta. Stocks with the worst historical tail losses tend to continue underperforming.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.73, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If CVaR is merely a longer-window volatility measure (corr with H176 > 0.90)

---

### H279 — CrashSensitivityRegime
**Signal:** Adaptive exposure based on portfolio skew state. Full exposure to low-downside-beta
names in all regimes. When VIX > 25, tighten to exclude bottom-quintile coskewness names
regardless of their momentum or quality rank.

**Why it works:** In calm markets, tail-risk signals have weak predictive power. During
stress, they matter greatly. A regime-adaptive tail filter should preserve return while reducing
left-tail contribution.

**Note:** This is a regime overlay strategy — similar in spirit to H066/H079. Expect wider fold variance.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.80, Max DD < −35%, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If regime switch fires too rarely to affect results (< 5% of trading days)

---

### H280 — SkewAdjustedMomentum
**Signal:** 12-1 month momentum with skewness penalty. Final score = momentum_rank −
0.4 × coskewness_rank − 0.3 × left_tail_rank. Long top-quintile.

**Why it works:** Pure momentum rewards continuation regardless of crash risk profile. Adding
skewness penalties removes names that would contribute disproportionate downside in a crash.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 1.00, Max DD < −30%, beats H157 (1.079)  
**Falsification:** If skewness adjustments reduce Sharpe without improving Max DD

## Batch 7 — H281–H285: Earnings Announcement Attention
**Source papers:** DellaVigna & Pollet (2009), Hong et al. (2000)  
**Theme:** Earnings announcements on Fridays or during low-attention periods generate delayed
price response — the drift window is longer for low-attention events.

**Data note:** Assume earnings announcement date is available (date-only, no intraday timestamp).
Weekday extracted from date. No intraday window feasible — use date-level drift.

### H281 — FridayEarningsDrift
**Signal:** For each quarterly earnings event, flag if announcement date is Friday. Compute
earnings surprise proxy (return on day of announcement vs. prior 21-day average return).
Enter long position on positive Friday surprise; hold for 30 trading days.

**Why it works:** Friday inattention means the market underreacts immediately; the drift window
is extended beyond typical PEAD. The positive expectation persists longer.

**Bias check:** Use actual announcement date, not fiscal period end. No look-ahead on surprise.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.70, CI lower > 0, meaningful trade count (N > 200 over 30 years)  
**Falsification:** If Friday effect is absent — Friday trades not meaningfully different from Mon–Thu

---

### H282 — AttentionAdjustedPEAD
**Signal:** Standard PEAD (long positive surprise stocks 1–30 days post-announcement), with
holding period extended to 60 days for small-cap-proxy names (bottom third by ADV as attention
proxy) and shortened to 20 days for high-ADV names.

**Why it works:** Information diffusion speed varies with visibility. Adjust holding period to
match how long the drift lasts in each attention segment.

**Note:** S&P 500 universe limits small-cap variation, but dollar volume still varies 10-fold
within index members.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.72, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If attention adjustment adds no incremental Sharpe vs. fixed-window PEAD

---

### H283 — VolumeContextEarningsDrift
**Signal:** PEAD filtered by post-announcement volume. Require that day-1 post-announcement
dollar volume is above median for the stock. Only enter positions with volume confirmation.

**Why it works:** Volume after the announcement confirms that the market is engaging with the
news. Low-volume reactions may be noise; high-volume reactions show real information is being
processed.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.75, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If volume gate eliminates too many events (< 50% survive) with no return improvement

---

### H284 — LowCoverageMomentumProxy
**Signal:** Use price efficiency as a coverage proxy: stocks with higher idiosyncratic volatility
relative to their own history (high IVOL/long-run-IVOL ratio) are treated as lower-coverage.
Apply standard 12-1 momentum but weight more heavily in low-coverage segment.

**Why it works:** Hong et al. (2000) show momentum is stronger where information diffuses slowly.
Without analyst count data, idio vol trajectory is a reasonable proxy for information environment.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.85, H157 corr < 0.85, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If the coverage proxy is too noisy to produce a stable IC in OOS folds

---

### H285 — EarningsAttentionComposite
**Signal:** Combine: (1) Friday surprise flag (weight 0.4), (2) abnormal post-announcement
volume (weight 0.3), (3) low dollar-volume attention proxy (weight 0.3). All components point
in the same direction: buy positive-surprise + inattention context. 30-day hold.

**Why it works:** Three independent inattention signals should produce a more reliable PEAD
composite than any single proxy. The goal is a robust attention-adjusted earnings drift.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.82, CI lower > 0, better than H282 standalone  
**Falsification:** If composite Sharpe falls below H282 (dilution confirmed)

## Batch 8 — H286–H290: Capital Discipline and Investment Efficiency
**Source papers:** Titman, Wei & Xie (2004), Richardson et al. (2005) deeper  
**Theme:** How firms allocate capital — whether disciplined or empire-building — predicts future
returns. Build the investment-discipline signal family.

### H286 — LowInvestmentGrowthFactor
**Signal:** Total asset growth YoY, sector-normalized. Rank ascending; long bottom-quintile
(lowest asset growth). Exclude utilities and REITs.

**Why it works:** Aggressive balance-sheet expansion often precedes poor returns. Management
overestimates investment opportunities; investors overpay for apparent growth.

**Note from program history:** H206 (Low Asset Growth) was already tested and kept (Sharpe 0.709).
H286 is the CAPEX-only variant — distinguishes capital expenditure growth from acquisitions.

**Signal:** Capex growth YoY (capex_t / capex_{t-4} − 1), sector-normalized. Rank ascending.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.72, H206 corr < 0.80, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If capex growth is empirically identical to total asset growth (corr > 0.90)

---

### H287 — InvestmentEfficiencyRatio
**Signal:** Revenue growth YoY / (capex-to-assets + 0.01). Higher ratio = more revenue per
unit of capital invested. Rank descending; long top-quintile. Sector-normalize.

**Why it works:** Pure capex growth is bad, but capex that generates revenue growth is good.
The efficiency ratio separates productive from unproductive investment.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.76, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If the ratio collapses to revenue growth alone (capex denominator adds nothing)

---

### H288 — ROICCapexDiscipline
**Signal:** Composite of return on invested capital (NOPAT / invested capital proxy) AND
low capex growth (H286 signal). Equal-weight z-scores; long top-quintile.

**Why it works:** Combines the quality of existing investments (ROIC) with the discipline
of not overinvesting (low capex growth). The best firms earn high returns on moderate investment.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.82, CI lower > 0, beats H286 standalone  
**Falsification:** If ROIC and capex growth have near-zero IC together (opposite regime behavior)

---

### H289 — AssetGrowthPenalty
**Signal:** Comprehensive investment activity: total asset growth + capex growth + acquisition
spending, all scaled and averaged. Rank ascending; long lowest-growth quintile.

**Why it works:** Empire-building can occur through multiple channels. A comprehensive investment
activity measure is more robust than any single component.

**Note from H206 (Low Asset Growth, Sharpe 0.709):** This extends H206 by adding capex and
acquisition components explicitly. If corr > 0.90 with H206, kill immediately.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.73, H206 corr < 0.88, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** Corr > 0.90 with H206 (confirmed duplicate)

### H290 — CapitalEfficiencyQualityComposite

## Batch 9 — H291–H295: Execution-Aware Low-Turnover Signal Design
**Source papers:** Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016)  
**Theme:** Transaction costs materially reduce anomaly profitability. Buy/hold spread logic and
turnover caps recover most of the gross alpha for monthly-rebalanced strategies.

### H291 — LowTurnoverValueFactor
**Signal:** Earnings yield (earnings/price) with a buy/hold band: buy when rank rises into
top-quintile AND rank is not already in top two-quintiles; sell only when rank falls out of
top-two-quintiles (wider sell threshold than buy threshold). This reduces round-trip turnover.

**Why it works:** Most value factors show heavy rank churn that destroys net alpha. The buy/hold
band trades less while maintaining similar gross exposure.

**Expected net improvement:** From gross Sharpe ~0.74 to net ~0.80 due to reduced round-trips.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.75, turnover < 60% one-way annualized, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If band causes so little trading that the portfolio drifts into value traps

---

### H292 — LowTurnoverQualityFactor
**Signal:** Gross profitability (H227 signal) with buy/hold band: buy top-quintile entry,
sell only on exit below top-two-quintiles. Rebalance quarterly instead of monthly.

**Why it works:** Quality signals are more stable than momentum signals — quarterly rebalancing
should not materially hurt gross alpha while cutting costs by ~75%.

**Benchmark:** H227 Sharpe 0.878 monthly. Target: quarterly version should be at least 0.85 net.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.85, turnover < 30% annually, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If quarterly rebalancing degrades gross Sharpe by > 10% (signal is too fast)

### H293 — CostAwareMomentumLongHold
**Signal:** 12-1 month momentum, rebalance every 2 months instead of monthly, with a 5%
score-change threshold required to replace a position. Long top-quintile.

**Why it works:** Momentum's turnover is its biggest cost driver. Extending the rebalance period
from monthly to bi-monthly reduces turnover ~40% with modest gross alpha reduction.

**Benchmark:** H157 vol-scaled momentum (Sharpe 1.079 monthly). Target: bi-monthly ≥ 1.00.

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 1.00, turnover < 100% one-way annually, CI lower > 0  
**Falsification:** If bi-monthly rebalancing misses momentum reversals that materially hurt OOS returns

**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ 0.88, turnover < 50% annually, CI lower > 0

## Key risks and flags

**Survivorship in distress signals (H256–H260):**  
S&P 500 constituents rarely go bankrupt inside the index. The distress signal may be weak.
If the average monthly exclusion rate < 2%, re-examine whether the signal has power here.

**R&D missing values (H266–H270):**  
Missing R&D in Compustat means zero for most non-tech firms. Do not impute non-zero values.
Report missing-field coverage by year and sector before drawing conclusions.

**Duplication risk:**  
H251 (idio vol) ↔ H176 (total vol): check corr before finalizing  
H276 (skewness) ↔ H197 (low skewness): may be near-duplicate  
H289 (asset growth) ↔ H206 (asset growth): cap corr threshold at 0.90 or kill H289  
H258 (distress change) — expect to underperform H256 (distress level) per program findings on level > change

**Dilution boundary:**  
Program history shows 3-factor composites (H264, H290, H298) sit at the dilution boundary.
Run single-factor versions first, then composites only if all components individually pass the keep bar.

**Execution-aware batch (H291–H295):**  
These strategies are methodological variants of existing signals, not new economic signals.
They can only be run after their parent signals have been run and kept.
