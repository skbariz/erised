### 1.3 Cross-Cutting Insights

**Insight 1: The H001 Correlation Floor**
No strategy in H001-H050 achieved H001 corr below +0.108 (H041). The minimum for a KEPT strategy was +0.334 (H043). The correlation/activity tradeoff is structural: strategies that fire only during H001's worst periods are most decorrelated but least active.

**Implication for H051+:** Genuine decorrelation requires either (a) cross-asset signals that fire in different economic regimes, or (b) higher-frequency operation that accumulates trades independently of SPY direction. The multi-asset families (H018-H022) have already shown that asset class expansion is the most reliable path to decorrelation. The path forward is deeper asset-class expansion.

**Insight 2: The CAGR-MaxDD Tradeoff**
Of the 16 kept individual strategies, 14 have lower CAGR than SPY (8.35%). The typical profile: Sharpe improvement comes from dramatically better Max DD, not higher CAGR. H021 (7.69% CAGR, −18.41% Max DD) vs. SPY (8.35% CAGR, −55.92% Max DD) is the prototype.

**Implication for H051+:** Do not target CAGR maximization. Target Sharpe/MaxDD improvement. Strategies with CAGR < SPY but much better Max DD are entirely expected and valuable.

**Insight 3: The Active-Period Sharpe Quality**
When strategies are active, their per-period Sharpe (conditional on being invested) is often very high. H043's fold Sharpes reach 1.85 in fold 9 and 1.48 in fold 6. The low OOS Sharpe comes from being flat for extended periods.

**Implication for H051+:** For calendar and regime-timing strategies, the OOS Sharpe denominator includes ALL time (including time spent flat). A strategy active 40% of days with true Sharpe 1.2 in active periods will report OOS Sharpe ~0.76. This is still very valuable.

**Insight 4: Data Availability Determines Fold Quality**
TLT available from 2002 means folds 0-1 (1993-2001 test windows) produce zero RiskParity returns. GLD from 2004 means fold 2 is also zero for GLD-dependent strategies. This structural gap lowers overall OOS Sharpe for strategies requiring these assets.

**Implication for H051+:** New assets (IEF from 2002, SHY from 2002, HYG from 2007, DBC from 2006, VNQ from 2004) will have similar gaps. Design strategies to degrade gracefully (go flat when data unavailable, as H021 already does) rather than fail completely.

**Insight 5: The Min-Corr Selection Principle**
H049 selected 4 strategies from 16 candidates by maximizing the minimum pairwise correlation constraint (all pairs < 0.50). The resulting portfolio outperforms weighting all 16 by Sharpe.

**Implication for H051+:** As new strategies are kept, continuously update the ensemble by re-running min-corr selection across ALL kept strategies (H001-H100). The final H099/H100 ensemble should include the best global portfolio, not just new additions.

### 1.4 Strategy Families NOT Yet Tested

These represent the highest-priority research directions, none of which appeared in H001-H050:

1. **Yield curve slope (10y-2y)** — arguably the most reliable macro recession predictor in finance. Harvey (1988) showed inverted curves precede recessions by 6-18 months. Never tested.

2. **Credit spread regime (HYG-LQD, Baa-Aaa)** — corporate credit markets are leading indicators of equity stress. Widening spreads precede equity declines. Never tested.

3. **Commodities as portfolio asset (DBC, oil)** — different inflation regime from bonds and gold. DBC has low or negative correlation with SPY in inflationary environments (2021-2022 demonstrated this).

4. **Real estate (VNQ/REITs)** — intermediate duration + equity-like dividends. Different regime behavior from both SPY and TLT.

5. **International equity vs. domestic (EEM, EFA)** — geographic diversification. EM vs. DM cycle is partially independent from US equity.

6. **Equity factor timing (value/growth/quality/size)** — the data is available via sector and factor ETFs. Cross-sectional factor rotation within equity is an entirely untested dimension.

7. **3+ asset risk parity** — H021 showed 2-asset parity works. The natural extension to 3-4 assets is obvious but untested.

8. **VIX term structure** — the VIX futures term structure (spot vs. 3-month VIX) has documented predictive power. Contango → calm; backwardation → stress.

9. **Earnings revision momentum** — analyst earnings revision cycles drive equity performance. Not tested because it requires point-in-time analyst estimate data.

10. **Macro composite signals** — combining yield curve + credit spread + VIX level into a single regime composite.

#### H051: Three-Asset Risk Parity (SPY + TLT + GLD)

**Hypothesis:**
H021 demonstrated that inverse-vol weighting of SPY and TLT achieves Sharpe 1.03 with Max DD −18.41%. Adding GLD as a third asset should further reduce portfolio vol during crises because GLD decorrelates from both SPY (negative real-rate driven) and TLT (inflation-driven) in distinct regimes. The 2008 crisis saw SPY crash and TLT rally; the 2021-2022 inflationary bear saw TLT crash while GLD held. A 3-asset portfolio captures both regimes.

**Signal:** Same as H021 (RiskParity) but allocate across 3 assets instead of 2. Weight each asset by its inverse 60d realized vol, normalized to sum to 1.0. No leverage.

**Data required:** SPY, TLT (2002+), GLD (2004+)
**Active folds:** 10 of 12 (folds 0-1 zero due to TLT/GLD unavailability; fold 2 partial for GLD)

**Key regimes:**
- 2000-2002 tech crash: no data, flat
- 2008-2009 GFC: SPY −50%, TLT +25%, GLD −5% → strategy holds mainly TLT, avoids SPY crash
- 2013-2017 low vol: SPY outperforms both → strategy holds mainly SPY (lower vol = higher weight)
- 2020 COVID: SPY −34% then +70%, TLT +20% → parity shifts to TLT then back to SPY
- 2021-2022 inflation: SPY −20%, TLT −35%, GLD +5% → all three decline; GLD weight increases

**Expected H001 corr:** +0.35 to +0.45 (lower than H021's +0.407 because GLD adds a third uncorrelated regime)
**Expected Sharpe:** 0.90-1.10 (likely similar to or slightly above H021)
**Success criteria:** OOS Sharpe > 0.90 with CI lower > 0.40; Max DD < −20%
**Falsification:** Sharpe < H021 or Max DD worse than H021 → GLD adds noise, not diversification

```python
class ThreeAssetRiskParity(MultiAssetStrategy):
    """H051: Vol-equalized risk parity across SPY, TLT, and GLD.

    Extends H021 (2-asset SPY+TLT) by adding GLD as a third asset.
    Each asset receives weight proportional to 1/realized_vol, normalized
    to sum to 1.0. Daily rebalancing.

    Requires: adj_close (SPY), tlt_close, gld_close columns.
    """
    name = "three_asset_risk_parity"

    def __init__(self, vol_lookback: int = 60):
        self.vol_lookback = vol_lookback

    def generate_weights(self, prices: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
        spy_r = prices["adj_close"].pct_change()
        tlt_r = prices["tlt_close"].pct_change() if "tlt_close" in prices else pd.Series(0.0, index=prices.index)
        gld_r = prices["gld_close"].pct_change() if "gld_close" in prices else pd.Series(0.0, index=prices.index)

        spy_vol = spy_r.rolling(self.vol_lookback, min_periods=self.vol_lookback).std().shift(1)
        tlt_vol = tlt_r.rolling(self.vol_lookback, min_periods=self.vol_lookback).std().shift(1)
        gld_vol = gld_r.rolling(self.vol_lookback, min_periods=self.vol_lookback).std().shift(1)

        # Inverse vol weights
        spy_w = 1.0 / spy_vol.replace(0, np.nan)
        tlt_w = 1.0 / tlt_vol.replace(0, np.nan)
        gld_w = 1.0 / gld_vol.replace(0, np.nan)

        # Zero out assets with no data
        tlt_avail = prices["tlt_close"].notna() if "tlt_close" in prices else pd.Series(False, index=prices.index)
        gld_avail = prices["gld_close"].notna() if "gld_close" in prices else pd.Series(False, index=prices.index)
        tlt_w = tlt_w.where(tlt_avail, 0.0)
        gld_w = gld_w.where(gld_avail, 0.0)

        total = spy_w + tlt_w + gld_w
        valid = total > 0
        w_spy = (spy_w / total).where(valid, 0.0).fillna(0.0)
        w_tlt = (tlt_w / total).where(valid, 0.0).fillna(0.0)
        w_gld = (gld_w / total).where(valid, 0.0).fillna(0.0)

        return pd.DataFrame({"SPY": w_spy, "TLT": w_tlt, "GLD": w_gld}, index=prices.index)
```

**Sensitivity parameters:** vol_lookback ∈ {20, 40, 60, 90, 120}

#### H054: IEF-TLT Duration Spread (Yield Curve Proxy)

**Hypothesis:**
The yield curve slope is the classic recession predictor (Harvey 1988). A flattening/inverting yield curve (short rates exceed long rates) predicts recessions with 6-18 month lead time. Without direct access to Treasury yield data, we can proxy the yield curve via relative performance of different duration bond ETFs: TLT (20yr) vs. IEF (7-10yr) vs. SHY (1-3yr). When short-duration bonds (SHY) outperform long-duration (TLT) on a total-return basis, this implies short rates are rising relative to long rates — yield curve flattening.

**Signal:** Long SPY when TLT/IEF ratio is below its 200MA (long rates not disproportionately elevated relative to mid-rates → curve is positive) AND IEF/SHY ratio is below its 200MA (mid-rate not elevated relative to short → not inverted); flat when either condition fails (curve flattening or inverting signal).

**Simplified version:** Long SPY when SHY < SHY-200MA (i.e., short bonds not in uptrend — short rates have not spiked upward). This approximates: short rates rising → yield curve problems.

**Data required:** SHY (2002+), IEF (2002+), TLT (already available)
**Active folds:** 10 of 12

**Expected H001 corr:** +0.45 to +0.65 (yield curve and momentum have overlapping regime signals)
**Expected Sharpe:** 0.55-0.80
**Expected Max DD:** −15 to −25% (yield curve inverted before 2000-2002, 2006-2008 — should reduce equity exposure in advance)

**Key regime behavior:**
- 1998-2000: Yield curve flatened → signal reduces equity → avoids 2000-2002 crash
- 2005-2007: Yield curve inverted → flat signal → avoids 2008 GFC (partial)
- 2010-2019: Yield curve positive → mostly long SPY
- 2022-2023: Yield curve deeply inverted → reduces equity during 2022 bear

**Falsification:** Yield curve inversion does not precede equity weakness in this dataset → relationship has broken down or the ETF proxy is too noisy

**Implementation blueprint:**
```python
class YieldCurveProxy(Strategy):
    """H054: Long SPY when yield curve (SHY-IEF-TLT spread) is not inverted.

    Proxies the 10y-2y yield curve using relative performance of bond ETFs.
    When short-duration bonds (SHY) are outperforming (rising short rates),
    the yield curve is flattening/inverting — a recession warning signal.
    Requires shy_close and ief_close columns.
    """
    def __init__(self, ma_period: int = 200):
        self.ma_period = ma_period
        self.name = f"yield_curve_proxy_{ma_period}d"

    def generate_positions(self, prices: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
        if "shy_close" not in prices.columns:
            return pd.Series(1.0, index=prices.index, name="position")
        shy = prices["shy_close"]
        ief = prices["ief_close"] if "ief_close" in prices else shy
        tlt = prices["tlt_close"] if "tlt_close" in prices else shy

        # Relative momentum as duration-spread proxy
        shy_mom = shy / shy.shift(63) - 1  # 3-month relative return
        tlt_mom = tlt / tlt.shift(63) - 1
        valid = shy_mom.notna() & tlt_mom.notna()

        # Yield curve flat/inverted: SHY outperforming TLT (short > long return)
        curve_inverted = (shy_mom > tlt_mom)
        pos = (~curve_inverted).astype(float)
        pos = pos.where(valid, 0.0)
        pos.name = "position"
        return pos
```

```python
class FiveAssetRotation(MultiAssetStrategy):
    """H055: Cross-sectional momentum rotation across 5 asset classes.

    Ranks SPY, TLT, GLD, DBC, VNQ by 12-1m return. Long the top-ranked
    asset if its return is positive; flat if all negative.
    Requires: tlt_close, gld_close, dbc_close, vnq_close columns.
    """
    name = "five_asset_rotation"

    def __init__(self, long_days: int = 252, skip_days: int = 21):
        self.long_days = long_days
        self.skip_days = skip_days

    def generate_weights(self, prices: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
        assets = {
            "SPY": prices["adj_close"],
            "TLT": prices.get("tlt_close", pd.Series(np.nan, index=prices.index)),
            "GLD": prices.get("gld_close", pd.Series(np.nan, index=prices.index)),
            "DBC": prices.get("dbc_close", pd.Series(np.nan, index=prices.index)),
            "VNQ": prices.get("vnq_close", pd.Series(np.nan, index=prices.index)),
        }
        mom = {k: (v.shift(self.skip_days) / v.shift(self.long_days) - 1) for k, v in assets.items()}

        # Build weight DataFrame: 1.0 in top momentum asset, 0 otherwise
        weights = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=prices.index, columns=list(assets.keys()))
        for i in range(len(prices)):
            day_mom = {k: mom[k].iloc[i] for k in mom if not pd.isna(mom[k].iloc[i])}
            if not day_mom:
                continue
            best = max(day_mom, key=day_mom.get)
            if day_mom[best] > 0:
                weights.iloc[i][best] = 1.0
        return weights
```

## APPENDIX C: Quick-Reference Bias Checklist for H051-H100

Before running ANY new strategy:

1. **Lookahead on new assets:** When joining IEF/SHY/HYG to the prices DataFrame, verify forward-filling is correct and column shifts are applied. A common bug: `prices["ief_close"].shift(1)` is correct; using `prices["ief_close"]` directly in a signal is lookahead.

2. **Data availability guard:** Each strategy class should return flat (position = 0) when required columns are NaN. The framework's walk_forward will produce fold results for periods where data doesn't exist; these should be near-zero Sharpe (acceptable) not errors.

3. **Walk_forward_multi dispatch:** For any MultiAssetStrategy, use `walk_forward_multi()` not `walk_forward()`. The dispatch pattern from H021's runner is the template.

4. **PCA lookahead (H096):** Rolling PCA must use only historical data. Use `.expanding().apply()` or precompute rolling covariance matrices with `min_periods` constraints. Never fit PCA on the full dataset and apply the global PC loadings — that's a form of lookahead.

5. **Yield curve proxy timing:** The yield curve signal (SHY vs. TLT momentum) is computed from past prices. Shift by 1 bar before using in a position decision. Standard `.shift(1)` convention applies.

6. **Credit spread joint data availability:** When both HYG and LQD are required but HYG starts later (2007), ensure the strategy defaults to a reasonable behavior for the HYG-unavailable period. The safest default: fall back to the simpler signal (e.g., H033 bond trend alone) when HYG is not available.
