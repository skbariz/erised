## Set 7 Thesis

**Three unsolved problems remain after 450 strategies:**

1. **The EDGAR gap.** Pure CS fundamental strategies produce zero returns in folds 0–5 (1993–2010)
   because EDGAR XBRL coverage begins ~2009. Only fundamental+price-momentum composites (H412,
   H415, H429, H447) get full 12-fold coverage by using the price signal to fill pre-EDGAR folds.
   Set 7 systematically exploits untested fundamental dimensions in this all-fold template.

2. **The MDR ceiling.** Terminal MDR has been stuck at 1.525 since H300 (52 strategies). Every
   expansion — H350 (80 strategies), H400 (99), H449 (31+14), H450 (56+14) — fails to improve.
   Root cause: CS strategies with EDGAR gaps contribute zero-correlation in folds 0–5, so the MDR
   optimizer cannot use them for cross-period diversification. The only escape is (a) more H194-class
   dollar-neutral L/S strategies with full 30-year price coverage, or (b) new all-fold fundamental
   templates that infiltrate the MDR pool alongside H043/H078/H035/H016.

3. **The L/S gap.** H194 (dollar-neutral weekly reversal, Sharpe 0.829, MaxDD −18.33%, H001
   corr +0.146) is the ONLY dollar-neutral strategy that works and the most valuable cross-program
   diversifier in H300. Long/short momentum (H193) was killed — too low signal strength per turn.
   The key insight: L/S requires HIGH per-period alpha to survive 3 bps/turn combined cost. Only
   high-frequency reversal signals (5-day hold period) qualify. Set 7 tests L/S versions of ALL
   major long-only reversal strategies (H185, H186, H189, H191, H192) that haven't been converted
   to L/S form. If even one survives, it becomes a second H194-class diversifier.

## Batch 1 — H451–H460: DuPont Level Signals & Untested Capital Return Ratios

**Source papers:** Fama-French (1992) multifactor decomposition; Novy-Marx (2013) gross profitability;
DuPont identity (NI/Rev × Rev/TA × TA/BE = ROE); Penman (2010) financial statement analysis.

**Thesis:** H201–H250 systematically tested YoY CHANGE versions of DuPont ratios. The LEVEL versions
are structurally different signals — persistent competitive position rather than recent improvement.
The program's consistent finding that level > change (GP/TA level 0.879 vs change 0.764) predicts
these level signals should outperform their change-based counterparts from H221-H250. Additionally,
three level signals have never been tested in any form (OCF/Revenue, GP/(TA-CA), GP/LTD).
The momentum-combo template (fundamental level + 6m price momentum) guarantees all-fold coverage
at ~Sharpe 0.96 with H001 corr ≈ 0.

### H451 — NetProfitMarginLevelPort
**Signal:** NI/Revenue (net profit margin) LEVEL. Sector-neutral, top 20%, quarterly rebalance.
Long persistent high-net-margin firms.  
**Why novel:** H238 tested YoY NI/Revenue CHANGE (0.696). The LEVEL of net profit margin captures
durable pricing power + cost structure efficiency. GP/Revenue level (H239: 0.861) was the program's
best solvency-batch signal; NI/Revenue includes SGA, interest, and taxes — captures the full cost
stack, not just gross margin. Firms with persistently high NI/Revenue have structural advantages
that resist competitive erosion.  
**Regime expectations:** 2000–2002: high-net-margin tech firms collapsed while margin expansion drove
the bubble; signal should avoid value traps. 2008–2009: financials with collapsing NI/Revenue dragged
down by writedowns — signal should reduce financial exposure naturally.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** NI/Revenue level duplicates GP/Revenue level signal (cross-sectional rank corr > 0.90).

### H452 — AssetTurnoverLevelPort
**Signal:** Revenue/TA (asset turnover) LEVEL. Sector-neutral, top 20%, quarterly rebalance.
Long high-structural-turnover firms.  
**Why novel:** H230 tested YoY Revenue/TA CHANGE (0.741). The LEVEL captures business model
efficiency: asset-light high-turnover firms (retailers, service companies) generate more revenue
per unit of capital. This is the second DuPont component (NI/Rev × Rev/TA × TA/BE = ROE) that
hasn't been tested as a level signal.  
**Regime expectations:** High-turnover business models (retail, software) tend to outperform
asset-heavy industries (manufacturing, utilities) across most macro regimes.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Revenue/TA level is dominated by sector membership rather than firm-specific quality.

### H453 — ReturnOnEquityLevelPort
**Signal:** NI/BE (return on equity) LEVEL. Sector-neutral, top 20%, quarterly rebalance.  
**Why novel:** H221 tested YoY ROE CHANGE (0.717). ROE LEVEL is fundamentally different: a firm
that has sustained high ROE for multiple periods has compounding structural advantages. ROE = ROA
× leverage multiplier — it rewards firms that efficiently deploy equity capital including financial
leverage (unlike pure GP/TA which ignores capital structure). Distinct from both H173 (ROA level:
0.785) and H221 (ROE change: 0.717).  
**Regime expectations:** High ROE firms tend to have strong balance sheets and competitive moats.
In 2008–2009, high-ROE firms with low leverage should outperform; high-ROE firms with high leverage
(e.g., financials) may underperform.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** NI/BE level is dominated by H246 (low financial leverage) signal — i.e., ROE is
just a proxy for deleveraged firms.

### H454 — CashProfitMarginPort
**Signal:** OCF/Revenue (operating cash flow margin) LEVEL. Sector-neutral, top 20%, quarterly.  
**Why novel:** Not tested in any prior hypothesis. OCF/Revenue captures cash conversion efficiency
of each revenue dollar — distinct from GP/Revenue (doesn't subtract SGA), OCF/TA (different
denominator), and OCF/BE (equity-adjusted). High OCF/Revenue means the firm converts revenue to
cash efficiently with minimal working capital trap.  
**Regime expectations:** 2020 COVID: firms with high OCF margins maintained operations while
revenue-heavy but cash-poor firms collapsed. Cyclical downturns punish low-OCF-margin firms first.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. MaxDD < −40%.  
**Falsified if:** OCF/Revenue merely duplicates GP/Revenue at sector-neutral level.

### H455 — GrossProfitFixedAssetsPort
**Signal:** GP/(TA-CA) = gross profit per unit of long-term/fixed assets. Sector-neutral, top 20%,
quarterly. Measures how much gross profit the firm's structural capital generates.  
**Why novel:** Not tested in any prior hypothesis in any form. Current assets (CA) cycle quickly;
long-term assets (TA-CA) are the structural capacity. GP/(TA-CA) rewards asset-light businesses
with high gross profit per fixed-cost investment — a proxy for economic moat strength. Distinct
from GP/TA (includes current assets in denominator) and GP/BE (equity-adjusted).  
**Regime expectations:** Capital-light, high-GP-per-fixed-asset firms (software, branded consumer)
outperform capital-heavy firms (manufacturing, mining) across regimes.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** GP/(TA-CA) ≈ GP/TA at sector-neutral level (CA variation insufficient cross-sectionally).

### H456 — GrossProfitDebtCoveragePort
**Signal:** GP/LTD (gross profit to long-term debt ratio) LEVEL. Sector-neutral, top 20%, quarterly.
Long firms whose gross profit substantially exceeds their long-term debt load.  
**Why novel:** H237 tested OCF/LTD (cash debt coverage: 0.729). GP/LTD uses the pre-SGA, pre-interest
gross profit line as coverage numerator — more structural than OCF (which varies with working capital).
GP/LTD = a firm's ability to retire all long-term debt from one year of gross profit; very high values
indicate financial fortress quality (like Altman's H244 but from the gross profit line).  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** GP/LTD merely selects zero-debt firms (large firms with zero LTD create NaN or Inf).
Handle: firms with LTD < 1% of TA are assigned neutral rank, not top rank.

### H457 — CashConversionEfficiencyPort
**Signal:** OCF/(CA-CL) = operating cash flow per unit of net working capital. Sector-neutral, top 20%,
quarterly. High ratio = firm generates strong cash from its operating cycle with minimal capital tied up.  
**Why novel:** Not tested in any form. Working capital (CA-CL) traps capital in receivables and
inventory. OCF/(CA-CL) measures the efficiency of that trap: how much operating cash flow is
generated per dollar of working capital. Distinct from OCF/Revenue (which measures margin) and
(CA-CL)/Revenue (which measures working capital intensity).  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Negative net working capital firms (CA < CL) dominate the ranking (handle by
excluding or clamping CA-CL at a small positive floor).

### H458 — NetMarginMomentumPort
**Signal:** z(NI/Revenue level) + z(6m price momentum). Equal weight. Monthly rebalance. All-fold.  
**Why novel:** The proven all-fold template (H412: 0.961, H415: 0.987, H429: 0.984, H447: 0.962):
any quality fundamental level + 6m price momentum → all 12 folds active, H001 corr ≈ 0, ~Sharpe 0.96.
NI/Revenue level (H451 standalone) hasn't been combined with price momentum. Net profit margin
captures the full cost stack (SGA, interest, taxes) — a quality dimension not yet used in this template.  
**Success criteria:** All 12 folds active, Sharpe ~0.96, H001 corr < 0.05.  
**Falsified if:** Sharpe < H451 standalone — momentum combo not helping (unlikely given template robustness).

### H459 — AssetTurnoverMomentumPort
**Signal:** z(Revenue/TA level) + z(6m price momentum). Equal weight. Monthly rebalance. All-fold.  
**Why novel:** Asset turnover level (H452 standalone) in the all-fold template. Tests whether asset
productivity (a distinctly operational quality signal) combined with price momentum produces a
different return profile than the margin-based templates (H415, H429 which use GP/Revenue).  
**Success criteria:** All 12 folds active, Sharpe ~0.96, H001 corr < 0.05.

### H460 — ROELevelMomentumPort
**Signal:** z(NI/BE level) + z(6m price momentum). Equal weight. Monthly rebalance. All-fold.  
**Why novel:** ROE level (H453 standalone) in the all-fold template. ROE includes financial leverage
(unlike GP/TA or GP/Revenue) — ROE + momentum selects firms with both sustainable equity returns
AND current price trend confirmation. Genuinely distinct from H231 (GP/TA + mom: 0.870) and H459.  
**Success criteria:** All 12 folds active, Sharpe ~0.96, H001 corr < 0.05.

## Batch 2 — H461–H470: Return Distribution Anomalies Beyond MAX Effect

**Source papers:** Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011) MAX effect; Ang et al. (2006) idiosyncratic vol;
Harvey & Siddique (2000) coskewness (already tested H277); Conrad, Dittmar & Ghysels (2013) return
distribution moments.

**Thesis:** The tail-risk family (H251–H280) covered MAX return avoidance (H252: 0.927), downside
beta (H253: 0.976), CVaR (H278: 0.911), and negative skewness (H276: 1.062). Untested:
minimum return (worst day crash risk), return consistency (fraction positive days), downside capture
ratio (realized response to market down days), beta stability, and vol-of-vol. All are pure
price-based signals with full 12-fold coverage and near-zero H001 correlation.

### H461 — MinReturnCrashRiskPort
**Signal:** MIN1 = minimum 1-day return in trailing 21 trading days. Long bottom 20% by |MIN1|
(avoid stocks with extreme crash days). Sector-neutral, monthly rebalance.  
**Why novel:** H252 tested MAX1 avoidance (avoid stocks with best day: 0.927). H461 tests MIN1
(avoid stocks with worst day). MIN1 captures the left-tail crash risk — stocks that had extreme
negative days have higher ongoing crash probability. Different from CVaR (H278, which uses average
of worst 5%) and downside beta (H253, which uses market-day regression).  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. Full 12 folds.

### H462 — ReturnAsymmetryPort
**Signal:** MAX1 − |MIN1|. High positive = upside-asymmetric (lottery-like). Long stocks with
negative or near-zero spread (symmetric or downside-safe). Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** Combines MAX1 and MIN1 into a net asymmetry measure. H252 avoids high MAX1; H461
avoids high |MIN1|. H462 targets the asymmetry BETWEEN them — capturing net return skew in the
short-term horizon. Not a linear combination of H252 and H461 because it measures the gap.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H463 — ReturnConsistencyPort
**Signal:** Fraction of positive return days over trailing 252 trading days. Long top 20% most
consistent (highest fraction positive days). Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** H225 tested earnings growth consistency (NI growth fraction). H405 tested cash
earnings persistence (OCF > NI fraction). H463 tests PRICE return consistency — a stock that has
positive returns more than 55% of days has lower drawdown risk and smoother compounding. Distinct
from H155 SmoothMomentum (which weights 12-1m momentum by path consistency — a multiplicative
interaction). H463 ranks independently on the consistency metric alone.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. Full 12 folds (pure price signal).

### H464 — DownsideCapturePort
**Signal:** Average daily return on days when SPY is negative (trailing 252 days), normalized by
own total vol. Long stocks with lowest downside capture (lose least on market down days). Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** H253 tested downside BETA (OLS regression slope vs SPY on down-days). Downside
capture is the empirical REALIZED response — it captures non-linear downside sensitivity that beta
regression misses (e.g., a stock with beta 0.8 but which spikes negatively only on large SPY
drawdowns has low regression beta but high downside capture). Structurally different signal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. H001 corr < 0.10 (pure idiosyncratic risk signal).

### H465 — UpsideSharpePort
**Signal:** (Mean positive-day return) / (total daily return std) over trailing 252 days. Long
top 20% by this upside-Sharpe ratio. Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** Standard Sharpe rewards both positive mean return AND low vol. Upside-Sharpe
rewards stocks that have more upside relative to their total risk — asymmetric return distribution.
Captures stocks that are "efficient" specifically on the upside, regardless of symmetric vs downside
behavior. Not equivalent to negated skewness (H276/H197) because it combines magnitude and frequency.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H466 — VolOfVolStabilityPort
**Signal:** Rolling standard deviation of monthly realized volatility (std of trailing twelve
21-day vol estimates over 252 days). Low vol-of-vol = stable risk profile. Long bottom 20%
(most stable). Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** H251 tested idiosyncratic vol LEVEL (0.897). H275 used vol in a composite
penalty (MomentumVolatilityScore). Vol-of-vol (stability of the volatility process) not tested
standalone. Firms with unstable vol have higher regime-change risk and lower institutional preference.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H467 — BetaStabilityPort
**Signal:** Rolling std of 63-day beta estimates computed quarterly over trailing 252 days (4
non-overlapping 63-day windows). Low beta-stability variance = predictable systematic risk.
Long bottom 20% by beta instability. Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** H175 (BAB) tests beta LEVEL (low beta = good). H253 tests downside beta. Beta
STABILITY (how predictable is the systematic risk exposure?) measures institutional forecastability.
Stable-beta stocks are easier to model → lower uncertainty premium → better expected returns.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H468 — ReturnAutocorrelationPort
**Signal:** First-order serial autocorrelation of daily returns over trailing 63 days. Long stocks
with POSITIVE autocorrelation (trend-continuers). Sector-neutral, monthly.  
**Why novel:** All existing momentum signals (H007, H150, H216 etc.) use CUMULATIVE formation-period
returns. H468 tests return AUTOCORRELATION — the degree to which yesterday's return predicts today's.
High positive AC = intrinsic momentum persistence within the stock's return series. Low/negative
AC = mean-reverting (reversal signal). Provides a direct measure of momentum "quality" at the
within-stock level.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H469 — MinReturnMomentumCombo
**Signal:** z(−MIN1) + z(6m momentum). Equal weight. Monthly rebalance. All-fold.  
**Why novel:** H255 TailFilteredMomentum tested z(mom) filtered by MAX1 avoidance (0.896 < H157
1.079 — the filter hurt). H469 combines MIN1 avoidance with momentum as EQUAL z-score composite
(not a filter). −MIN1 selects stocks without crash risk days; +momentum selects recent winners.
Distinct: H255 used MAX1 as filter (exclusion); H469 uses MIN1 as z-score signal (ranking).  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H157 vol-scaled momentum (1.079). All 12 folds active.

### H470 — ReturnConsistencyMomentumCombo
**Signal:** z(fraction positive days, 252d) + z(6m momentum). Equal weight. Monthly. All-fold.  
**Why novel:** H155 SmoothMomentum = 12-1m momentum × fraction_positive_days (path-consistent,
multiplicative). H470 = z(consistency fraction) + z(6m mom), additive z-score, independently ranked.
Different construction: H155 uses consistency as a momentum MODIFIER; H470 treats consistency as
an independent alpha signal. Tests whether return consistency adds a separate dimension to momentum.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, all 12 folds active, H001 corr < 0.10.

## Batch 3 — H471–H480: Dollar-Neutral Long-Short Market-Neutral Extensions

**Source:** Avellaneda & Lee (2010) stat arb; Lo & MacKinlay (1990) contrarian L/S; all prior
long-only reversal strategies (H185, H186, H189, H191, H192).

**Thesis:** H194 (dollar-neutral L/S weekly sector reversal: Sharpe 0.829, MaxDD −18.33%, H001
corr +0.146) is the program's most valuable cross-program diversifier and the only dollar-neutral
strategy that works. H193 (12-1m L/S momentum) was killed — insufficient signal strength at
monthly rebalance to overcome 3 bps/turn combined L/S cost.

The viability condition: weekly (5-day) reversal generates ~0.4–0.6% gross weekly alpha from
the market microstructure reversal effect. At 3 bps/turn combined = ~1.56% annual drag. Net
alpha ~2–3% annually → Sharpe ~0.7–1.0. This math works ONLY for high-frequency reversal signals.

**Target: find a second H194-class diversifier.** Even one additional dollar-neutral L/S keeper
could push the MDR ceiling past 1.525 by adding a genuinely orthogonal return stream.

**Implementation notes:**
- Dollar-neutral: position dollar amounts in long leg = position dollar amounts in short leg
- Long leg: top 20% by signal within sector; Short leg: bottom 20% by signal within sector
- Sector-neutral within each leg separately (not just net)
- Cost: 1.5 bps/turn each way = 3 bps/turn total (same underlying cost model)
- These are pure price-signal strategies — FULL 12-fold coverage, no EDGAR gap

### H471 — LongShortSectorReversal
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of within-sector 5-day reversal (H185 strategy in L/S form).
Long prior 5-day within-sector losers (top 20%); short prior 5-day within-sector winners
(bottom 20%). Weekly rebalance.  
**Why:** H185 long-only sector reversal = Sharpe 1.103. H194 (the baseline L/S that works) is
generic cross-stock reversal; H471 is specifically WITHIN-SECTOR reversal in L/S form. Within-sector
reversal has stronger IC per period (sector-level mean reversion is tighter) than cross-sector. If
signal strength is retained in L/S form, H471 should achieve H194-class Sharpe.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. H001 corr < 0.25. Full 12 folds.

### H472 — LongShortResidualReversal
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of idiosyncratic 5-day reversal (H186 strategy in L/S form).
Long stocks with lowest 5-day idiosyncratic return (strip SPY beta); short highest. Weekly.  
**Why:** H186 long-only residual reversal = Sharpe 1.095. Stripping SPY beta before computing
the reversal signal removes systematic noise — the idiosyncratic component should have higher
signal-to-noise ratio. L/S amplifies the idiosyncratic reversal further.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. H001 corr < 0.20.

### H473 — LongShortPCAReversal
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of PCA residual 5-day reversal (H191 in L/S form). Strip K=10
PCA factors from daily returns, compute 5-day residual return, fade (reverse). Weekly.  
**Why:** H191 long-only PCA reversal = Sharpe 1.135 — highest long-only reversal Sharpe. PCA
stripping removes up to 10 common factors, leaving the purest idiosyncratic signal. L/S of this
should have near-zero correlation with everything.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. H001 corr < 0.20.

### H474 — LongShortOUReversal
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of OU mean reversion (H189 in L/S form). Compute OU z-score on
5-day idiosyncratic return (entry when z < −1.5 or z > +1.5). Long negative-z stocks; short
positive-z. Weekly position refreshes.  
**Why:** H189 long-only OU reversion = Sharpe 1.025. The OU process provides an explicit model
for mean reversion speed — only enter positions when the displacement from mean is statistically
significant. This selectivity should improve L/S cost efficiency vs threshold-free reversal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. Full 12 folds.

### H475 — LongShortPCAOUReversal
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of PCA OU mean reversion (H192 in L/S form). K=10 PCA, OU
z-score on 5-day cumulative residuals. Weekly.  
**Why:** H192 long-only PCA OU = Sharpe 1.175 — the BEST long-only short-term signal in the
entire program. PCA stripping + OU selectivity combined. If ANY L/S strategy should survive the
cost hurdle, this is the leading candidate.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.70, CI lower > 0.25. H001 corr < 0.20. This is the primary
MDR ceiling crack attempt.

### H476 — LongShortBABWeekly
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S BAB at weekly rebalance. Long bottom 20% by 252-day realized
beta vs SPY; short top 20%. Equal dollar exposure. Weekly beta refresh.  
**Why:** H175 long-only BAB = Sharpe 1.088. The Frazzini-Pedersen (2014) pure L/S BAB factor
achieves higher Sharpe than long-only because it eliminates the market premium and extracts pure
beta-arbitrage alpha. Weekly rebalance keeps beta estimates fresh. Beta updates slowly enough that
the week-to-week signal is stable.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H477 — LongShortNegativeSkewness
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of negative skewness avoidance (H276 in L/S form). Long stocks
with most negative realized 252-day skewness (avoid lottery); short stocks with most positive
skewness. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** H276 long-only negative skewness = Sharpe 1.062. Skewness is a monthly-updated signal
(stable enough for monthly L/S rebalance). The short leg (positive skewness stocks = lottery
stocks) should underperform — their lottery premium is overpaid. Monthly L/S: 12 turns × 3 bps
= 0.36% annual cost — manageable if gross L/S alpha > 1%.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H478 — LongShortQualityWeekly
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of GP/TA quality signal (H227 signal) rebalanced WEEKLY. Long
top 20% GP/TA; short bottom 20%. Weekly.  
**Why:** H227 long-only GP/TA monthly = Sharpe 0.879. Testing whether WEEKLY rebalance of a
monthly-updated fundamental signal provides enough per-period signal to overcome L/S costs. GP/TA
updates quarterly (forward-filled daily) — the weekly L/S exploits the CROSS-SECTIONAL DISPERSION
in GP/TA which is stable and large. Risk: the fundamental signal doesn't change fast enough for
weekly L/S to add over monthly.  
**Likely outcome:** Probably killed (low per-period signal variation in fundamentals). Included to
confirm the threshold where L/S becomes viable.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.

### H479 — LongShortShortMomentum
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S of 3-month (63-day, skip 0) price momentum. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** H193 (12-1m L/S momentum, monthly) was killed. The hypothesis here: shorter-horizon
momentum (3-month vs 12-month) has higher per-period IC and decays less between monthly rebalance
dates. 3m momentum has stronger cross-sectional signal per month (faster forming = less look-through).
Monthly: 12 turns × 3 bps = 0.36% annual cost. If gross 3m-L/S alpha exceeds 1%, this survives.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. This is a falsification test — if even 3m L/S
fails at monthly, the L/S momentum space is truly closed at this cost structure.

### H480 — LongShortCompositeReversal
**Signal:** Dollar-neutral L/S composite signal: z(5-day sector-neutral reversal) + z(−MAX1_5d).
Short stocks that are: (1) recent 5-day winners AND (2) had an extreme positive day in that window
(lottery premium). Long stocks that are: (1) recent losers AND (2) had no extreme days. Weekly.  
**Why:** Combines the sector reversal signal (H471) with the lottery premium (H252) in a single
L/S signal. The combination should have higher per-period IC than either alone: a 5-day winner
that also had a MAX day is more likely to reverse (lottery overshoot); a 5-day loser with no
extreme days is more likely to recover (no overshooting).  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H471 (LongShortSectorReversal). H001 corr < 0.20.

## Batch 4 — H481–H490: Hybrid AA+CS — Aggregate Fundamental Signals for ETF Timing

**Completely untested strategy class.** No prior hypothesis in H001–H450 used aggregated
cross-sectional fundamental data to time an AA (ETF-level) allocation decision.

**Thesis:** The cross-sectional fundamentals panel contains macro-level information when aggregated
across the S&P 500 universe. Market-wide GP/TA (corporate sector quality), market-wide accruals
(earnings quality risk), and cross-sectional return dispersion (ETF vs active market regime) are

### Key implementation rules

**CS strategies (all except Batches 4 partial, 5 partial, 7 partial):**
- `_sector_neutral_weights`, quarterly default (63d); monthly where specified
- Fundamental signals: forward-filled from Compustat filing dates; no additional shift needed
- Market cap = `prices_panel × _align_fund(shares_outstanding, prices_panel)`, 63d lag
- GP/(TA-CA): clip CA to [0, TA] before subtraction; handle TA-CA < 0 (rare) by excluding
- GP/LTD: handle LTD ≈ 0 by assigning neutral rank (not top rank) to avoid Inf signals
- NI/BE: handle negative BE (highly leveraged or distressed) by assigning neutral/bottom rank

**L/S strategies (Batch 3: H471–H480):**
- Dollar-neutral: `long_weights - short_weights`, net = 0
- Long leg: top 20% by signal; short leg: bottom 20%; sector-neutral within each leg
- Cost is 1.5 bps each way = 3 bps/turn combined; apply same cost_model as H194
- Check n_trades — expected ~2× long-only counterpart (trading both legs)
- Use H194 as implementation template (the reference L/S strategy that works)

**Hybrid AA+CS strategies (Batch 4: H481–H490):**
- Aggregate CS fundamentals: compute daily cross-sectional median/std from the fundamentals panel
- Use 252-day trailing average of the daily cross-sectional metric for smoothing (avoids noise)
- Scale factor: map the smoothed metric to [0.7, 1.3] exposure range (not hard binary switches)
- H021 base: apply the scale factor to the SPY weight only; TLT/GLD weights adjust inversely
- Use `walk_forward` or `walk_forward_multi` per the base strategy type

**All-fold requirement check:**
- A strategy is "all-fold" if and only if Sharpe is reported for all 12 folds with N > 0 trades
- Momentum templates (Batch 1 H458-H460, Batch 2 H469-H470): price component guarantees all folds
- Pure fundamental signals (H451-H457, H461-H467): expect folds 0-5 ≈ 0 (EDGAR gap)
