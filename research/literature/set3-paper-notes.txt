S&P 500 QUANT PAPER LEARNINGS - SET 3
Third non-overlapping set of 20 papers for planning 10 additional batches of trading algorithms

Purpose:
This file is a chatbot-ready research brief. It summarizes another 20 quant trading and
financial modeling papers that were not used as the core papers in Set 1 or Set 2, then turns
those papers into 10 practical development batches for S&P 500 strategy research.

Important:
This is research and software-planning material, not financial advice. Every strategy must be
tested with point-in-time data, realistic costs, realistic borrow assumptions where shorts are
used, and out-of-sample validation.

================================================================================
HOW SET 3 IS DIFFERENT FROM SET 1 AND SET 2
================================================================================

Set 1 focused on the core engine:
- momentum, trend, value, quality, profitability, volatility management, earnings drift,
  liquidity-aware reversal, pairs/stat-arb, and machine-learning ranking.

Set 2 focused on additional cross-sectional signals:
- industry momentum, residual momentum, short-term reversal, seasonality, corporate issuance,
  analyst/insider/short-seller signals, options-informed signals, attention, and sentiment.

Set 3 focuses on a different layer:
- tail-risk and lottery-stock avoidance
- distress and bankruptcy-risk modeling
- balance-sheet sustainability
- capital investment discipline
- accrual reliability
- R&D and intangible-asset valuation
- customer-supplier information diffusion
- analyst-coverage-limited momentum
- volume/visibility effects
- Friday earnings announcement inattention
- attention-grabbing stock pressure
- news and 10-K text tone
- S&P 500 index addition/deletion effects
- mutual-fund flow pressure
- trading-cost-aware anomaly implementation

The goal of Set 3 is not just to add more signals. The goal is to make the strategy factory more
selective, more data-aware, and more realistic about which anomalies survive execution.

================================================================================
IMPLEMENTATION WARNINGS FOR THIS SET
================================================================================

1. Many Set 3 signals require harder data than prices.
   Examples:
   - CRSP/Compustat-style fundamentals
   - SEC filing timestamps
   - customer-supplier links
   - analyst coverage
   - daily news text
   - mutual fund holdings and fund flows
   - S&P index change dates

2. Use point-in-time availability.
   A financial statement dated December 31 is not available on December 31. Use filing dates,
   announcement dates, or conservative reporting lags.

3. Keep S&P 500 universe logic honest.
   Use point-in-time constituents. Do not test today's S&P 500 list backward through history.

4. Treat shorting as optional.
   Many papers document long-short anomalies. For a practical S&P 500 system, build both:
   - long-only ranking/avoid overlays
   - long-short research versions with borrow/cost assumptions

5. Cost control is central in Set 3.
   Several signals are short-horizon or event-driven. Use no-trade bands, buy/hold spreads,
   turnover caps, and impact models before trusting results.

6. Do not let text/news models become a token sink.
   Store compact precomputed text features:
   - negative_tone
   - uncertainty
   - litigiousness
   - modal_weak
   - modal_strong
   - article_count
   - abnormal_news_count
   - filing_tone_change

01. Ang, Hodrick, Xing, and Zhang (2006)
Title: The Cross-Section of Volatility and Expected Returns
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w10852

Core idea:
Stocks with high idiosyncratic volatility have unusually low future average returns, and this
cannot be explained away by standard size, value, momentum, or liquidity controls.

Trading translation:
High idiosyncratic volatility should usually be treated as a warning flag, especially when the
stock also has weak quality, high distress, high MAX return, or poor profitability.

Features to implement:
- idio_vol_21d, idio_vol_63d, idio_vol_252d
- residual volatility from regression on SPY plus sector ETF
- residual volatility from regression on Fama-French-style proxies
- idio_vol_rank_within_sector
- idio_vol_change_1m

Strategy ideas:
- Long-only avoid overlay: remove top decile idiosyncratic volatility stocks unless quality and
  momentum are both strong.
- Defensive factor sleeve: rank stocks by low idio vol plus high profitability.
- Momentum cleaner: penalize momentum signals that are mostly high-volatility lottery moves.

Main caveats:
- Idiosyncratic volatility is correlated with distress, size, price, and lottery demand.
- S&P 500 names are large and liquid, so the effect may be weaker than in all-stock universes.
- Avoid using contemporaneous factor residuals that would not be known at rebalance time.

02. Bali, Cakici, and Whitelaw (2011)
Title: Maxing Out: Stocks as Lotteries and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w14804

Core idea:
Stocks with extreme recent positive daily returns, measured by the maximum daily return over the
past month, tend to have lower subsequent returns. The paper connects this to investor preference
for lottery-like payoffs.

Trading translation:
A recent extreme one-day winner is often not a clean momentum stock. It may be an overbought
lottery stock.

Features to implement:
- max_daily_return_21d
- max_daily_return_63d
- max_return_rank_within_sector
- max_return_minus_realized_vol
- max_return_interaction_with_news_attention

Strategy ideas:
- Lottery avoid list: underweight top-decile MAX stocks.
- Momentum quality filter: only buy high-momentum stocks if MAX is not extreme.
- Reversal candidate: fade high MAX stocks after abnormal retail attention or weak fundamentals.

Main caveats:
- Some high MAX events are real information events, such as takeover rumors or earnings surprises.
- Avoid blindly shorting high MAX stocks around merger/news risk.
- Use event filters before treating high MAX as pure overpricing.

03. Harvey and Siddique (2000)
Title: Conditional Skewness in Asset Pricing Tests
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2980335

Core idea:
Skewness and coskewness matter for asset pricing. Investors care not only about variance, but also
about whether an asset worsens bad tail outcomes.

Trading translation:
Build a tail-shape overlay. Some stocks look attractive by mean/volatility but contribute badly to
portfolio skew or crash behavior.

Features to implement:
- rolling_skew_63d, rolling_skew_252d
- downside_beta_252d
- coskew_with_spy_252d
- left_tail_loss_252d
- drawdown_sensitivity_to_spy_down_days

Strategy ideas:
- Crash-aware portfolio construction: penalize names with high downside beta and poor skew.
- Tail-balanced long book: combine momentum/value names with skew-aware constraints.
- Regime overlay: tighten exposure to bad-skew names when market volatility rises.

Main caveats:
- Skew estimates are noisy.
- Daily data may not be enough for stable single-stock coskew estimates.
- Treat this more as a risk control than a standalone alpha signal.

04. Campbell, Hilscher, and Szilagyi (2008)
Title: In Search of Distress Risk
Source: https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/3199070?show=full

Core idea:
Financially distressed stocks, measured with a dynamic failure-probability model using accounting
and market variables, have historically earned low returns despite high risk.

Trading translation:
Distress is often not compensated in equities. High failure probability is usually a reason to
avoid a stock, not to demand a higher expected return.

Features to implement:
- chs_distress_score
- leverage, market_equity, cash_to_assets, profitability, recent_return
- volatility and drawdown inputs
- distress_rank_within_sector
- distress_change_qoq

Strategy ideas:
- Distress avoid overlay for all long-only strategies.
- Quality-value filter: buy cheap stocks only when distress score is acceptable.
- Short/underweight research sleeve: high distress plus negative momentum.

Main caveats:
- Bankruptcy prediction models require careful point-in-time accounting.
- Distressed stocks can squeeze violently.
- S&P 500 distressed names may exit the index, so constituent survivorship is a big risk.

05. Ohlson (1980)
Title: Financial Ratios and the Probabilistic Prediction of Bankruptcy
Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v18y1980i1p109-131.html

Core idea:
Ohlson applies a conditional logit framework to predict corporate failure using financial ratios.
The resulting O-score is a classic accounting-based distress measure.

Trading translation:
Use O-score-like variables as slow-moving balance-sheet distress inputs, then combine them with
market-based distress from Campbell-Hilscher-Szilagyi.

Features to implement:
- o_score
- total_liabilities_to_total_assets
- working_capital_to_total_assets
- current_liabilities_to_current_assets
- net_income_to_total_assets
- funds_from_operations_to_total_liabilities
- size-adjusted total assets

Strategy ideas:
- Fundamental distress filter: avoid high O-score stocks.
- Distress trend model: penalize stocks where O-score worsens across filings.
- Value trap detector: cheap valuation plus bad O-score equals lower priority.

Main caveats:
- Accounting definitions must match the model carefully.
- Financial firms may need separate treatment.
- Bankruptcy models are classification models, not direct expected-return models.

06. Hirshleifer, Hou, Teoh, and Zhang (2004)
Title: Do Investors Overvalue Firms with Bloated Balance Sheets?
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=404120

Core idea:
Net operating assets, roughly cumulative operating earnings minus cumulative free cash flow,
negatively predict future returns. High net operating assets can indicate balance-sheet bloat and
unsustainable accounting performance.

Trading translation:
Avoid companies where accounting earnings look good but cash generation does not support them.

Features to implement:
- net_operating_assets_to_assets
- operating_assets_minus_operating_liabilities
- cumulative_accrual_proxy
- NOA_rank_within_sector
- NOA_change_yoy

Strategy ideas:
- Sustainability quality factor: long low NOA, avoid high NOA.
- Value trap filter: cheap stocks with high NOA require extra confirmation.
- Earnings quality overlay: penalize high accruals plus high NOA.

Main caveats:
- Accounting field construction is tedious.
- Sector differences matter.
- Financials and regulated utilities may need special handling.

07. Titman, Wei, and Xie (2004)
Title: Capital Investments and Stock Returns
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w9951

Core idea:
Firms that substantially increase capital investment tend to have lower future abnormal returns.
The paper argues investors may underreact to empire-building implications of aggressive investment.

Trading translation:
High investment growth is not automatically good. It can be a red flag unless supported by
profitability, returns on capital, and strong demand.

Features to implement:
- capex_to_assets
- capex_growth_yoy
- investment_growth_3y
- capex_minus_depreciation_to_assets
- investment_rank_within_sector

Strategy ideas:
- Conservative investment factor: prefer lower, disciplined capital investment.
- Avoid aggressive investment after strong past returns.
- Quality-investment composite: high ROIC plus disciplined capex.

Main caveats:
- Some sectors require high capex structurally.
- Growth firms can rationally invest heavily.
- Use sector normalization and profitability interaction.

08. Richardson, Sloan, Soliman, and Tuna (2005)
Title: Accrual Reliability, Earnings Persistence and Stock Prices
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165410105000406

Core idea:
Accrual components differ in reliability. Less reliable accruals are less persistent, and investors
can misprice the persistence of earnings components.

Trading translation:
Do not treat all earnings equally. Cash earnings and reliable accruals deserve more weight than
less reliable accruals.

Features to implement:
- working_capital_accruals
- noncurrent_operating_accruals
- financial_accruals
- total_accruals_to_assets
- cash_earnings_to_assets
- accrual_reliability_score

Strategy ideas:
- Earnings quality rank: high cash earnings, low unreliable accruals.
- PEAD filter: positive earnings surprise only counts strongly when accrual quality is high.
- Value filter: cheap plus reliable earnings beats cheap plus aggressive accruals.

Main caveats:
- Requires high-quality accounting definitions.
- Annual and quarterly versions can disagree.
- Treat missing fields conservatively.

09. Chan, Lakonishok, and Sougiannis (2001)
Title: The Stock Market Valuation of Research and Development Expenditures
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=195488

Core idea:
The market can underappreciate R&D investment because it is expensed rather than capitalized. R&D
intensity has predictive value, especially in certain growth or beaten-down subsets.

Trading translation:
Build intangible-aware value and quality features. Some firms that look expensive on accounting
book value may be investing heavily in intangible assets.

Features to implement:
- rd_to_sales
- rd_to_market_equity
- rd_to_assets
- rd_growth_yoy
- intangible_adjusted_book_to_market
- rd_intensity_rank_within_industry

Strategy ideas:
- R&D value: high R&D intensity plus reasonable valuation.
- R&D recovery: high R&D intensity after poor past returns, if distress is low.
- Intangible-adjusted value factor for tech, healthcare, and communications.

Main caveats:
- Missing R&D is not always zero R&D.
- R&D has sector-specific meaning.
- Some R&D spend is wasteful; combine with profitability and revenue growth.

10. Cohen and Frazzini (2008)
Title: Economic Links and Predictable Returns
Source: https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Economic-Links-and-Predictable-Returns

Core idea:
News about customers can predict future returns of suppliers because investors underreact to
publicly available customer-supplier links.

Trading translation:
Build customer momentum. If a major customer stock has a large positive or negative move, supplier
stocks may drift afterward.

Features to implement:
- customer_weighted_return_1m
- customer_weighted_return_3m
- supplier_customer_link_strength
- top_customer_sales_share
- customer_news_shock
- customer_earnings_surprise_proxy

Strategy ideas:
- Supplier momentum: buy suppliers after positive customer shocks.
- Supplier warning: avoid suppliers after negative customer shocks.
- Link-confirmed momentum: strengthen a supplier's own momentum when customer momentum agrees.

Main caveats:
- Customer-supplier data is difficult and may be stale.
- Public customer lists often cover only major customers.
- Many S&P 500 firms are both customers and suppliers in complex networks.

11. Hong, Lim, and Stein (2000)
Title: Bad News Travels Slowly: Size, Analyst Coverage, and the Profitability of Momentum Strategies
Source: https://stein.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/bad-news-travels-slowly-size-analyst-coverage-and-profitability-momentum-strategi

Core idea:
Momentum profits are consistent with gradual information diffusion. Momentum is stronger where
information travels more slowly, such as lower analyst coverage stocks.

Trading translation:
Momentum should be conditioned on information diffusion. For S&P 500 stocks, use relative analyst
coverage, news density, ownership breadth, and complexity as diffusion proxies.

Features to implement:
- analyst_coverage_count
- coverage_rank_within_sector
- news_count_63d
- information_diffusion_score
- momentum_x_low_coverage
- negative_news_momentum

Strategy ideas:
- Low-coverage momentum: overweight momentum where information diffusion is slower.
- Bad-news drift avoid: underweight negative momentum names with sparse coverage.
- Coverage-adjusted holding period: slower diffusion gets longer holding period.

Main caveats:
- S&P 500 names usually have more coverage than small stocks.
- Coverage data availability may be expensive.
- Analyst coverage can proxy for size/liquidity, so control for those.

12. Lee and Swaminathan (2000)
Title: Price Momentum and Trading Volume
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=92589

Core idea:
Trading volume helps identify where a stock sits in its momentum life cycle. Volume interacts with
past returns and can help explain continuation versus reversal behavior.

Trading translation:
Do not use price momentum alone. Add volume state to distinguish early accumulation, crowded
momentum, exhaustion, and reversal risk.

Features to implement:
- turnover_21d
- turnover_252d_percentile
- abnormal_volume_21d
- momentum_12_1
- momentum_x_volume_state
- volume_trend_3m

Strategy ideas:
- Early momentum: high price momentum without extreme crowded volume.
- Crowded momentum warning: strong momentum plus extreme turnover and high MAX gets lower weight.
- Reversal watchlist: high past return, high volume, weakening trend.

Main caveats:
- Volume meanings change across eras due to market structure.
- ETF/index trading can distort single-name volume.
- Use dollar volume and turnover rather than raw shares.

Title: The High-Volume Return Premium
Source: https://scholars.duke.edu/individual/pub773086

Core idea:
Stocks experiencing unusually high trading volume can earn higher subsequent returns, consistent

Trading translation:
A positive volume shock can be a visibility signal, especially when not accompanied by obvious

Main caveats:

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/11683.html

Friday earnings announcements have less immediate response and more delayed response, consistent

Trading translation:

Main caveats:

- Avoid lookahead from earnings databases that revise dates.

Core idea:
Individual investors are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks, such as stocks in the news,

Trading translation:
Attention can create temporary buying pressure. Combined with MAX and weak fundamentals, attention

Features to implement:

- abnormal_volume_flag
- news_presence_flag
- attention_x_MAX
- attention_x_quality

Strategy ideas:
- Attention reversal: fade attention spikes in low-quality, high-MAX names.
- Attention confirmation: allow breakout continuation only when quality/news context supports it.
- Retail-pressure risk overlay: avoid chasing stocks with attention but no fundamental support.

Main caveats:
- Attention is not always bearish.
- The original setting studies investor behavior, not a guaranteed return anomaly.
- S&P 500 stocks can attract both retail and institutional attention.

================================================================================
NEW STRATEGY PRIMITIVES FROM SET 3
================================================================================

Primitive 1: Lottery-risk avoidance
- Penalize high idiosyncratic volatility, high MAX, bad skew, and weak fundamentals.

Primitive 2: Distress avoidance
- Combine Ohlson-style accounting distress with Campbell-Hilscher-Szilagyi market distress.

Primitive 3: Balance-sheet sustainability
- Prefer low net operating assets, low unreliable accruals, and strong cash earnings.

Primitive 4: Conservative investment
- Penalize aggressive capex/investment growth unless profitability and growth quality justify it.

Primitive 5: Intangible-aware value
- Adjust value and quality for R&D intensity, especially in tech, healthcare, and communications.

Primitive 6: Information diffusion
- Momentum is more useful when information diffusion is slow or when related-firm news has not
  been fully incorporated.

Primitive 7: Volume/visibility state
- Use abnormal volume and turnover to decide whether a move is early, crowded, or exhausted.

Primitive 8: Attention pressure
- Attention can create short-term buying pressure and later reversal in low-quality lottery names.

Primitive 9: Text tone
- Use finance-specific news and filing tone as compact features, not as raw text dumped into prompts.

Primitive 10: Flow and index pressure
- Index membership and fund flow pressure can create demand-driven moves separate from fundamentals.

Primitive 11: Execution-aware anomaly survival
- Every signal needs cost, turnover, capacity, and no-trade-band versions.

BATCH 1: Tail Risk, Lottery Risk, and Defensive Avoidance
Source papers:
- Ang, Hodrick, Xing, and Zhang (2006)
- Bali, Cakici, and Whitelaw (2011)
- Harvey and Siddique (2000)
- Campbell, Hilscher, and Szilagyi (2008)

Objective:
Create a risk-avoidance layer that improves every long-only strategy by removing stocks with
bad tail, lottery, and distress profiles.

Algorithms:
1. Low idiosyncratic volatility quality sleeve.
2. High-MAX lottery avoid overlay.
3. Downside-beta and skewness portfolio constraint.
4. Tail-risk composite: idio vol + MAX + downside beta + distress.

Required data:
- Daily adjusted OHLCV.
- Sector classifications.
- SPY or market proxy.
- Fundamental data for distress interactions.

Implementation tasks for Claude Code:
- Build `features_tail_risk.py`.
- Add rolling idio-vol, MAX, skew, downside beta, and tail-loss calculations.
- Create `tail_risk_score` from standardized components.
- Backtest as both standalone ranking and overlay on Set 1 momentum/value strategies.
- Save compact results to `results/set3_batch1_tail_risk_summary.json`.

Validation:
- Confirm all rolling features are lagged one trading day.
- Compare results with and without sector neutralization.
- Report turnover impact when used as an avoid overlay.

BATCH 2: Fundamental Distress and Value-Trap Detection
Source papers:
- Campbell, Hilscher, and Szilagyi (2008)
- Ohlson (1980)

Objective:
Build a robust distress model that prevents cheap-but-dangerous stocks from contaminating value,
contrarian, and high-yield strategies.

Algorithms:
1. O-score accounting distress.
