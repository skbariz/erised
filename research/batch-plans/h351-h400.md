## Batch 1 — H351–H355: Q-Factor Investment & Profitability Signals

**Source papers:** Hou, Xue, and Zhang (2015) *Digesting Anomalies*

**Thesis:** The q-factor model shows that investment and profitability are the two most economically central return predictors. Low investment-to-assets (capital discipline) and high ROE are persistent cross-sectional signals computable from EDGAR. Using them as first-principles controls purifies downstream signals.

**Regime expectations:**
- 2000–2002: low investment / high ROE screens avoid capex-heavy tech bubble names → large alpha
- 2008–2009: high ROE firms more likely to survive; low investment signals balance sheet health
- 2020 COVID: quality firms recover faster; low investment firms have less financial stress
- 2013–2017 low-vol: signal weaker as cheap-money era inflates mediocre firms

### H351 — QInvestmentRankPortfolio
**Signal:** Rank S&P 500 stocks by investment-to-assets (ΔAssets / lag_Assets, annual). Long lowest-quartile (capital-disciplined firms). Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Low asset growth predicts higher returns in academic literature; low investment signals management restraint and financial health.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0, MaxDD < -40%.  
**Falsified if:** Signal reverses (high investment earns more) or CI clips zero.

### H352 — QROELevelPortfolio
**Signal:** Rank by trailing twelve-month ROE (net income / book equity). Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance to reduce turnover on a slow-moving accounting signal.  
**Why:** Profitability (ROE) is the second q-factor pillar; persistent high ROE predicts future returns.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** ROE rank provides no incremental value over GP rank (H165/H239 baseline).

### H353 — QInvestProfComboPortfolio
**Signal:** Equal-weight z-score composite of low investment rank + high ROE rank. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** The q-factor model says both signals work together; combining them should reduce noise.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > max(H351, H352), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Composite underperforms either component (signal dilution).

### H354 — QResidualMomentumPortfolio
**Signal:** 12-1 price momentum residualized on q-factor exposures (investment + profitability ranks). Regress monthly cross-sectional momentum score on investment rank and profitability rank; take residual. Long top residual quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** McLean-Pontiff shows momentum weakens post-publication; stripping q-factor contamination keeps the structural part of the signal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H196 (idiosyncratic momentum baseline 0.852+), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Residualization destroys signal entirely (momentum was mostly q-factor).

### H355 — QFactorMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H351, H352, H353, H354, plus H239 (GP level) and H227 (gross profitability). SLSQP optimizer, same walk-forward windows.  
**Why:** q-factor signals are correlated with each other but should diversify against reversal/attention signals.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.80, CI lower > 0, MaxDD better than constituent average.  
**Falsified if:** MDR collapses to one signal with near-zero weights on others.

## Batch 2 — H356–H360: Composite Mispricing Signals

**Source papers:** Stambaugh and Yuan (2017) *Mispricing Factors*

**Thesis:** Many anomalies cluster into two composites: management-related mispricing (issuance, accruals, asset growth) and performance-related mispricing (earnings surprise, return-on-assets drift). Treating them as groups rather than independent signals reduces dimensionality and multiple-testing risk.

**Regime expectations:**
- 2000–2002: management mispricing (issuance, accruals) peaks before bubble burst → strong signal
- 2008–2009: performance mispricing (ROA drift, earnings surprise) resets violently → signal noise
- 2020 COVID: management mispricing signal weakens as all issuance explodes
- 2013–2017: both signals moderate; quality overall earns less in low-vol environments

### H356 — ManagementMispricingPortfolio
**Signal:** Equal-weight composite of (a) net stock issuance rank, (b) accruals rank (low = better), (c) asset growth rank (low = better). Z-score and rank. Long top decile (least management mispricing). Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** These three signal families all proxy for management actions that dilute shareholder value; the composite should be more stable than any one signal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Individual components already captured by H207 (shareholder yield) and H211 (accruals).

### H357 — PerformanceMispricingPortfolio
**Signal:** Equal-weight composite of (a) ROA drift (QoQ change in ROA), (b) earnings surprise persistence (3-period PEAD score), (c) operating cash flow rank. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Performance mispricing is corrected when positive fundamentals eventually translate into price; the composite smooths noise in any single metric.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** No incremental value over H183 (cash earnings drift) or H184 (earnings momentum composite).

### H358 — MispricingComboPortfolio
**Signal:** 50/50 blend of ManagementMispricing + PerformanceMispricing composite scores. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** The two mispricing composites are economically distinct and should diversify within the cross-sectional signal family.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > max(H356, H357), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Blend underperforms constituent (orthogonality assumption wrong for S&P 500).

### H359 — MispricingAvoidFilter
**Signal:** Start from equal-weight S&P 500 universe; exclude bottom decile on combined mispricing score (worst management + worst performance mispricing). Hold remaining stocks equal-weight. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** A long-only avoid filter (not full L/S) should retain more Sharpe while keeping MaxDD manageable.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, MaxDD < -35%, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Exclusion filter has no effect on Sharpe vs equal-weight benchmark.

### H360 — MispricingMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H356, H357, H358, H359, H194 (LongShortReversal as anchor). SLSQP.  
**Why:** Mispricing composites should diversify against the reversal-dominant MDR ensembles from H300/H350.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 1.0, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** MDR still collapses to H194 with no weight on mispricing signals.

## Batch 3 — H361–H365: Nonlinear & Regularized Characteristic Models

**Source papers:** Freyberger, Neuhierl, and Weber (2020) *Dissecting Characteristics Nonparametrically*; Kozak, Nagel, and Santosh (2020) *Shrinking the Cross-Section*

**Thesis:** Linear z-scores assume that a stock in the 95th percentile of GP is proportionally better than one at 70th. Nonlinear scoring (percentile bins, monotonicity checks) can extract the shape of the return relationship. Shrinkage prevents extreme weight assignments in high-dimensional composites.

**Regime expectations:**
- Nonlinear models capture the "extreme is different" effect that linear scoring misses
- Shrinkage most valuable in periods with limited EDGAR coverage (folds 6–8)
- 2020 COVID: extreme percentile bins fire strongly for quality tails

### H361 — PercentileBinnedGPPortfolio
**Signal:** Divide GP/assets into quintile bins (not continuous z-score). Assign rank score 1–5 to each bin. Long top bin (quintile 5). Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** The return-to-GP relationship may be convex; the top quintile may be far superior to quintile 4 even though linear z-scores don't reflect that.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H239 (GP level baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Quintile 5 earns similar to quintile 4 (linear model adequate).

### H362 — MonotonicityTestedCompositePort
**Signal:** For each of 5 signals (GP, ROE, accruals, asset growth, momentum), test monotonicity of quintile returns in the training window. Use only signals that show monotonic quintile pattern (each quintile strictly better/worse than prior). Combine passing signals into equal-weight composite. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Freyberger shows monotonicity violations in raw signals are common and indicate noise. Filtering to monotonic signals should improve OOS stability.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H234 (multi-factor composite baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Monotonicity filter is too restrictive (removes all signals in some folds).

### H363 — ShrunkReturnForecastPortfolio
**Signal:** In each training window, fit an elastic-net cross-sectional regression of next-month return on 8 characteristics (GP, ROE, accruals, asset growth, momentum 12-1, earnings surprise, book-to-price, leverage). Apply L2 shrinkage (alpha=0.5). Generate shrunk expected-return rank. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Kozak et al. show shrinkage is essential when many characteristics are tested together; raw OLS overweights noisy signals.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H199 (multi-signal composite), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Elastic-net degenerates to equal weights (all signals equally noisy).

### H364 — NonlinearMomentumBinPort
**Signal:** 12-1 momentum divided into decile bins. Assign nonlinear score: bins 1–3 = -1 (sell), bins 4–7 = 0 (neutral), bins 8–10 = +1 (buy). Long top bins only. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Momentum is known to have a "flat middle" — the return relationship is U-shaped or J-shaped. Forcing linear scoring misses the fact that middle-momentum stocks earn nothing.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H150 (momentum long-only baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Nonlinear binning doesn't improve over linear momentum.

### H365 — ShrunkCompositeEnsemblePort
**Signal:** Take 6 best cross-sectional strategies from H351–H364 by training-window Sharpe. Compute shrunk optimal weights (ridge-penalized mean-variance with lambda=2). Apply weights to strategy returns. Rebalance quarterly.  
**Why:** Kozak shows that penalized weight assignment prevents the MDR-style optimizer from concentrating on one signal that happened to work in the training window.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 1.0, MaxDD < -30%, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Shrinkage collapses to equal weight (no improvement over equal-weight ensemble).

## Batch 4 — H366–H370: Anomaly Decay & Incremental Alpha Controls

**Source papers:** McLean and Pontiff (2016) *Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?*; Green, Hand, and Zhang (2017) *The Characteristics that Provide Independent Information*

**Thesis:** Published anomalies weaken after publication and after 2003 when quants began systematically exploiting them. Strategies should either use signals with demonstrated recent-period strength, or apply explicit post-publication decay haircuts to avoid overfitting to stale academic anomalies.

**Regime expectations:**
- Post-2010: only signals with genuine structural basis survive arbitrage pressure
- Pre-2010: academic signals earn more (less crowded); post-2010 haircuts bite hard
- EDGAR fold 6+ only: decay controls matter most in the period where we have data

### H366 — RecentEpochOnlyPortfolio
**Signal:** Identical to H239 (GP level rank) but scored only on the most-recent 3 folds of the training window (post-2010 data). Older folds down-weighted 50%. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Green-Hand-Zhang show post-2003 predictability is weaker; a strategy that adapts its signal weights to recent-epoch evidence should be more realistic.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, more stable fold variance than H239 baseline, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Down-weighting early folds destroys statistical power with no Sharpe benefit.

### H367 — PostPublicationHaircutPort
**Signal:** Composite of 6 academic signals (GP, accruals, asset growth, momentum, earnings surprise, ROA). Each signal's z-score multiplied by a post-publication decay factor: 0.7 for signals published pre-2005, 0.85 for published 2005–2015, 1.0 for published after 2015. Long top adjusted-composite quartile.  
**Why:** McLean-Pontiff show ~26% decay post-publication on average. Explicitly haircut-ting known signals avoids overfitting to pre-publication returns.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H199 (multi-signal composite baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Haircut reduces Sharpe below un-haircut version (signals still earn pre-publication strength).

### H368 — IncrementalAlphaFilterPort
**Signal:** For each of 8 candidate signals, run cross-sectional regression of forward return on signal + GP + momentum controls each training window. Keep only signals with t-stat > 1.5 on incremental coefficient. Weight passing signals equally. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Feng-Giglio-Xiu show most new factors fail to add incremental information after existing factors are controlled for; the filter should leave only genuinely independent signals.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.60, stable across folds, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Too few signals pass in most windows (filter too strict for S&P 500's 500-stock universe).

### H369 — EigenportfolioCharacteristicPort
**Signal:** PCA on monthly cross-sectional characteristic matrix (GP, ROE, accruals, asset growth, momentum, leverage, earnings surprise). Use first 3 principal components as synthetic signals. Long top quartile on PC1 score (rotates to maximum cross-sectional variance direction). Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Green-Hand-Zhang show few characteristics provide independent information; PCA extracts the truly independent dimensions. This is a simplified proxy for the IPCA approach.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0, stable PC1 interpretation across folds.  
**Falsified if:** PC1 flips sign across folds (factor instability).

### H370 — DecayMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H366, H367, H368, H369 plus H239 and H265 (filter+rank baseline). SLSQP walk-forward.  
**Why:** Decay-adjusted signals should have lower inter-strategy correlation than raw signals; MDR should find more diversification.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 1.0, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** MDR concentrates weight on one signal and ignores the rest.

## Batch 5 — H371–H375: ML Model Ladder (Linear → Elastic Net → Gradient Boosting)

**Source papers:** Chen, Pelger, and Zhu (2023) *Deep Learning in Asset Pricing*; Freyberger et al. (2020); Kozak et al. (2020)

**Thesis:** ML should be a model ladder, not a first move. Linear cross-sectional rank → elastic net → gradient boosting. Only escalate to the next level if the prior level is clearly beaten out of sample. For S&P 500's 500-stock universe, complex models have limited statistical power; baseline comparisons are mandatory.

**Implementation note:** All models trained strictly on training folds. No hyperparameter tuning on test folds. Features: GP, ROE, accruals, asset growth, 12-1 momentum, earnings surprise, leverage, book-to-price (8 features max).

### H371 — LinearCrossSectionalRankModel
**Signal:** OLS cross-sectional regression of next-month return on 8 characteristics (no regularization). Rank predicted returns. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** The required linear baseline. If linear OLS already does the job, elastic net and GB add no value. Also establishes the baseline Sharpe for the ML ladder.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. This is the floor; expect H372+ to beat it.  
**Falsified if:** OLS rank does not even match equal-weight top-GP quartile.

### H372 — ElasticNetRankPortfolio
**Signal:** Elastic net regression (alpha=0.3, L1_ratio=0.5) of next-month return on 8 characteristics. Walk-forward: fit on training folds, predict test fold. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Regularization reduces overfitting in the small-N (500 stocks) environment. Elastic net selects the most predictive subset of signals automatically each fold.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H371 (linear baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Elastic net collapses to equal-weight (all signals have similar regularized coefficients).

### H373 — GradientBoostingRankPortfolio
**Signal:** LightGBM or sklearn GradientBoostingRegressor fit on the same 8-feature matrix. Shallow trees (max_depth=3, n_estimators=100). Rank predicted scores. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** GB can capture nonlinear interactions (e.g., high GP only matters when momentum is not negative). The depth limit prevents overfitting in S&P 500's small cross-section.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H372 (elastic net), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** GB overfits to training windows and OOS Sharpe < elastic net.

### H374 — MLEnsembleRankPortfolio
**Signal:** Equal-weight blend of predicted ranks from H371 + H372 + H373. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Model averaging smooths overfitting from any single model. If the three models disagree on a stock, the ensemble is appropriately neutral.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > max(H371, H372, H373), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Ensemble underperforms best individual model (blend introduces noise).

### H375 — MacroStateConditionedMLPort
**Signal:** H373 (gradient boosting rank) with an additional macro-state feature: whether SPY is above/below 200-day MA (regime = 1/0). The GB model sees 9 features total. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Chen-Pelger-Zhu show macro state conditioning improves deep-learning asset pricing models. A simple macro state flag should help the GB model avoid long-only equity exposure in down-trend periods.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H373, MaxDD < H373, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Macro state feature adds noise (GB ignores or misuses it OOS).

## Batch 6 — H376–H380: Earnings-Event-Aware Signal Amplification

**Source papers:** Engelberg, McLean, and Pontiff (2018) *Anomalies and News*; Tetlock, Saar-Tsechansky, and Macskassy (2008) *More Than Words*

**Thesis:** Anomaly payoffs are concentrated around earnings announcement dates. A position that is sized larger during the earnings window and reduces in quiet periods should earn more of the true alpha with less tracking noise. We use EDGAR filing dates as the earnings news event (the point-in-time anchor available in our data).

**Implementation note:** Use the quarterly EDGAR filing date as the "earnings event window." Define a ±5-day window around each filing date as the event window. This is computable from our existing data without external news APIs.

### H376 — EarningsWindowQualityBoost
**Signal:** H239 (GP level rank) but position size is 1.5× normal weight in the ±5-day earnings filing window and 0.8× in quiet periods. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance base, event-scale daily.  
**Why:** Engelberg et al. show quality anomaly payoff is concentrated around earnings; scaling up in the window captures more signal with same average exposure.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H239 (1.861+ based on prior results), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Earnings-window boost increases MaxDD without improving Sharpe (event risk dominates).

### H377 — EarningsSurpriseDriftAmplified
**Signal:** PEAD (post-earnings announcement drift) signal from H183/H184 baseline, but size scaled by surprise magnitude rank. Larger surprises get larger position weights. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Earnings drift is stronger for larger surprises; nonlinear sizing on the surprise signal should improve the risk-adjusted return.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H183 or H184 baseline, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Scaling amplifies drawdown proportionally (MaxDD worsens as much as Sharpe improves).

### H378 — QuietPeriodMomentumPort
**Signal:** 12-1 momentum rank, but only hold positions in the 3-week "quiet window" between earnings dates (exclude ±5-day earnings window entirely). During earnings windows, go to cash for those names. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Momentum earns little on earnings days (price impact already incorporated) and has gap risk. Avoiding the event window should reduce MaxDD without destroying the quiet-period alpha.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H150 (momentum baseline), MaxDD improvement, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Quiet-period momentum earns nothing (all momentum alpha was on event days).

### H379 — EarningsConfirmedQualityPort
**Signal:** GP level rank (H239) filtered through earnings confirmation: only hold stocks where earnings surprise in the most recent filing was non-negative. Exit positions when earnings disappoint. Long top quartile of GP rank with positive earnings confirmation. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Tetlock et al. show positive news tone confirms fundamental quality signals. Earnings confirmation acts as a news-tone proxy (positive surprise = positive fundamental news language).  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.65, CI lower > 0, MaxDD < -30%.  
**Falsified if:** Earnings confirmation filter too restrictive (removes too many top-GP stocks to maintain diversification).

### H380 — EventAwareMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H376, H377, H378, H379, plus H239 and H265 anchors. SLSQP walk-forward.  
**Why:** Event-aware signals should have lower mutual correlation than the raw fundamental signals they are derived from.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 1.0, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Event-aware modifications add no incremental value vs non-event versions in MDR.

## Batch 7 — H381–H385: Governance Proxy Signals

**Source papers:** Gompers, Ishii, and Metrick (2003) *Corporate Governance and Equity Prices*; Bebchuk, Cohen, and Ferrell (2009) *What Matters in Corporate Governance?*; Edmans (2011) *Employee Satisfaction and Equity Prices*

**Thesis:** Good governance can be proxied from EDGAR data without a governance database. The three best available proxies are: (1) share dilution rate (frequent share issuers dilute shareholders → bad governance), (2) buyback quality (firms returning capital via buybacks demonstrate shareholder focus), (3) management compensation efficiency (proxied via SG&A-to-revenue ratio as admin overhead signal).

### H381 — LowDilutionGovernancePort
**Signal:** Annual dilution rate = (shares_outstanding_t - shares_outstanding_t-1) / shares_outstanding_t-1. Long stocks in lowest quartile dilution (minimal share issuance). Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Frequent share issuers signal management extracting value from shareholders; low dilution is a strong governance proxy available from EDGAR since the 1990s.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Dilution proxy already fully captured by H207 (shareholder yield).

### H382 — BuybackQualityGovernancePort
**Signal:** Rank by buyback yield (buybacks-to-market-cap, annual) with interaction filter: only count buyback yield as positive governance if not simultaneously issuing stock (net buyback = buybacks minus new issuance > 0). Long top net-buyback quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Bebchuk et al. show entrenchment is about specific management actions. Net buyback directly measures management returning capital vs extracting it.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H209 (buyback momentum baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** No improvement over raw buyback yield (H209).

### H383 — CapitalDisciplineGovernancePort
**Signal:** Composite of (a) low dilution rank, (b) high net buyback rank, (c) low SG&A/revenue rank (management overhead efficiency). Equal-weight z-score composite. Long top quartile. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Combines three independent governance proxies; each alone is noisy, composite is more robust.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > max(H381, H382), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** SG&A component destroys the governance signal (SG&A varies too much by industry to be useful without sector-neutral adjustment).

### H384 — GovernanceQualityCompositePort
**Signal:** H383 composite × quality filter: only include stocks where GP rank > 50th percentile (governance must coexist with fundamental quality to predict returns). Long top quartile on governance composite, quality-screened. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Edmans shows governance predicts returns especially when human capital and fundamentals are also strong. The governance × quality interaction should be more powerful than governance alone.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.65, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Quality filter too restrictive or governance proxy adds no lift over pure quality.

### H385 — GovernanceMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H381, H382, H383, H384, H265, H239. SLSQP walk-forward.  
**Why:** Governance proxy signals have low correlation with pure quality/momentum signals; MDR should weight them meaningfully.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 1.0, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** MDR assigns <5% weight to all governance signals (completely dominated by quality anchors).

## Batch 8 — H386–H390: ESG & Controversy Proxy Signals

**Source papers:** Hong and Kacperczyk (2009) *The Price of Sin*; Bolton and Kacperczyk (2021) *Do Investors Care about Carbon Risk?*; Pastor, Stambaugh, and Taylor (2022) *Dissecting Green Returns*

**Thesis:** Without ESG vendor data, we proxy exclusion pressure and controversy with GICS sector classification. The "sin" proxy is the tobacco/alcohol/weapons sector. "High carbon" proxy is energy sector. "Green" proxy is utilities + technology with low capex intensity. These are coarse but unambiguous proxies computable from point-in-time sector data.

**Important caveat:** Sector-based ESG proxies measure sector membership, not firm-level ESG scores. Results will be noisier than vendor ESG data. These strategies test whether sector-exclusion dynamics affect returns on S&P 500 — not whether individual-firm ESG scores predict.

### H386 — SinExclusionPremiumTest
**Signal:** Compare equal-weight portfolio of S&P 500 GICS sector 15 (tobacco/staples sub-sector) vs equal-weight remaining S&P 500. This is a diagnostic test strategy — not a long-short (excluded sectors too small for full L/S). Long-only S&P 500 with 0% weight to sin proxy sector.  
**Why:** Hong-Kacperczyk show sin stocks earn higher expected returns due to institutional exclusion. For S&P 500, the effect is muted — this tests whether it's detectable at large-cap.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0. Even if low Sharpe, a diagnostic finding is valuable.  
**Falsified if:** No detectable difference between sin-excluded and full-S&P portfolios.

### H387 — LowCarbonSectorNeutralPort
**Signal:** Sector-neutral quality rank, excluding energy sector (GICS sector 10). Within-sector ranking on GP/assets. Long top quartile within remaining sectors, equal-weight across sectors. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Bolton-Kacperczyk show high-emission firms earn a risk premium that can reverse under climate policy shocks. Excluding energy and doing sector-neutral quality captures the carbon-risk-free quality signal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H239 (GP level baseline), MaxDD < H239 MaxDD, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Energy exclusion has no effect on Sharpe or MaxDD vs non-excluded version.

### H388 — GreenProxyQualityPort
**Signal:** Long-only on "green proxy" stocks: utilities (GICS 55) + tech companies (GICS 45) with capex/revenue < 10th percentile of their sector. These proxy for asset-light, low-carbon-intensity businesses. Weight by GP rank within the green proxy universe. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** Pastor-Stambaugh-Taylor show green stocks earn repricing gains when ESG preferences shift. Asset-light tech + utilities is our best available proxy without carbon data.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.51, CI lower > 0, distinct return pattern from H239 (low H001 corr expected).  
**Falsified if:** Green proxy universe too small (<30 stocks in most periods) to provide diversified signal.

### H389 — ControversyProxyAvoidPort
**Signal:** Avoid stocks in the bottom decile of combined controversy proxy score: (high leverage rank + high dilution rank + low GP rank). These three conditions together proxy for distressed governance. Long remaining stocks weighted by GP. Quarterly rebalance.  
**Why:** High leverage + dilution + low profitability is the closest we can get to a controversy/ESG-red-flag without vendor data. Avoiding them as a quality + governance filter.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H239 baseline, MaxDD < -35%, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Controversy proxy already captured by existing quality screens (H244 Altman-Z proxy).

### H390 — ESGProxyMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H386, H387, H388, H389, H239, H265. SLSQP walk-forward.

## Batch 9 — H391–H395: Volume-Based Attention & Disagreement Signals

**Source papers:** Antweiler and Frank (2004) *Is All That Talk Just Noise?*; Chen, De, Hu, and Hwang (2014) *Wisdom of Crowds*

**Thesis:** Without social media data, we proxy attention and disagreement with volume data. Abnormal volume (volume relative to trailing 60-day average) is the most robust publicly available attention proxy. Volume disagreement (intraday high-low range divided by volume) proxies for investor disagreement. These are well-validated signals in the academic microstructure literature.

### H391 — LowAttentionValuePort
**Signal:** Long-only strategy: rank stocks by GP/assets (value proxy) × (1 - abnormal_volume_rank). Low attention + high GP = "neglected quality." Abnormal volume = trailing 20-day volume / trailing 60-day volume. Long top quartile. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Antweiler-Frank show social attention concentrates on high-momentum names. Neglected quality stocks may be underfollowed and slowly repriced without attention risk.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H239, H001 corr potentially lower (neglect signal uncorrelated with attention-driven momentum), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Neglect proxy adds noise (volume has no relationship to GP mispricing speed).

### H392 — VolumeConfirmedMomentumPort
**Signal:** 12-1 momentum rank filtered: only hold momentum stocks with above-median volume trend (20-day/60-day volume ratio > 1.0). This is the volume-confirmation version of momentum.  
**Why:** Chen et al. show crowd-confirmed signals are stronger than raw signals. Volume confirmation of price momentum proxies for conviction in the crowd signal.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H150 (momentum baseline), CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Volume confirmation selects "chased" names, adding reversal risk.

### H393 — HighVolumeDisagreementAvoid
**Signal:** Portfolio of S&P 500 stocks with position weights inversely proportional to volume disagreement proxy (range/volume). High range + high volume = high disagreement → reduce weight. GP-rank weighted, disagreement-penalized. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Antweiler-Frank show message-board disagreement predicts volatility. High disagreement stocks have wider future return distributions — disagreement-penalized weighting should improve Sharpe.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > H239, lower volatility than GP baseline, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Range/volume ratio too noisy at daily frequency to provide stable monthly signal.

### H394 — AbnormalVolumeRiskFilter
**Signal:** Start from H265 (filter+rank composite). Apply risk filter: if a stock's abnormal volume z-score exceeds +2.0 in the current month, reduce its position weight by 50% (attention spike = event risk flag). Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** High attention spikes (abnormal volume > 2σ) correlate with earnings gaps, short-squeeze dynamics, and M&A speculation — all sources of uncompensated risk for fundamentals-based positions.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ H265 with improved MaxDD, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Reducing high-volume names removes the best-performing positions (momentum names that attracted volume).

### H395 — AttentionMDRPortfolio
**Signal:** MDR ensemble over H391, H392, H393, H394, H265, H239. SLSQP walk-forward.  
**Why:** Volume-attention signals are derived from price data, not EDGAR; they have a distinct signal timing (daily vs quarterly). MDR should find their orthogonality to fundamental signals.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 1.0, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Volume signals get near-zero MDR weight consistently (volume adds no information beyond fundamentals).

## Batch 10 — H396–H400: Execution Refinement + Terminal Ensemble

**Source papers:** Hendershott, Jones, and Menkveld (2011) *Does Algorithmic Trading Improve Liquidity?*; + Grand synthesis

**Thesis:** The last batch refines execution assumptions and builds the terminal MDR ensemble. Execution improvement means adjusting position sizes based on estimated transaction cost, prioritizing low-turnover signal versions, and modifying the spread model for the cross-sectional program. H400 is the grand terminal MDR over the strongest H351–H399 strategies plus the best prior-program strategies.

### H396 — LowTurnoverGPPortfolio
**Signal:** GP/assets rank, but rebalanced only when a stock's rank changes by more than 2 deciles (threshold rebalancing). Average rebalance frequency drops to ~4-6x/year from 12x. Track actual vs threshold-triggered turnover in stats.  
**Why:** Hendershott et al. show S&P 500 large-cap execution is cheap in normal conditions, but cumulative turnover costs still compound. Threshold rebalancing captures most of the signal while halving turnover.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ H239 with ≤60% of H239 turnover, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Threshold rebalancing degrades Sharpe by >0.05 (signal decays too fast to hold).

### H397 — SpreadAwarePositionSizer
**Signal:** H265 (filter+rank composite) but position weights scaled by inverse of estimated effective spread proxy: weight_i ∝ rank_score_i / spread_proxy_i, where spread_proxy = (daily_high - daily_low) / close, trailing 20-day average. Normalize weights to sum to 1. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** Hendershott shows effective spread varies even within S&P 500. Overweighting low-spread names in the same fundamental-rank group captures more signal per unit of execution cost.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ H265, cost-adjusted improvement, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** High-spread names within the same quality rank are the actual alpha generators (perhaps distressed quality).

### H398 — LiquidityRegimeCapPort
**Signal:** H374 (ML ensemble rank) with a liquidity regime cap: if rolling 20-day average dollar volume for a stock drops below the 10th percentile of its trailing 1-year distribution, cap the position at 50% of its normal weight. Risk cap only, not a complete exit.  
**Why:** Even large-cap S&P 500 stocks can have liquidity events (halts, earnings blackout, M&A). The cap prevents outsized positions when execution risk rises.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe ≥ H374 with MaxDD improvement, CI lower > 0.  
**Falsified if:** Liquidity cap fires too frequently and reduces diversification below the benefit of avoiding illiquid events.

### H399 — ExecutionOptimizedCompositePort
**Signal:** Equal-weight blend of H396, H397, H398. Monthly rebalance.  
**Why:** The three execution-aware versions of core signals should diversify across different cost models.  
**Success criteria:** Sharpe > 0.80, CI lower > 0, lower estimated turnover cost than H265/H374.  
**Falsified if:** Execution refinements add complexity without improving net-of-cost Sharpe.

### H400 — UltimateGrandMDR400
